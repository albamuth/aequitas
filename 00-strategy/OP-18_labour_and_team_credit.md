# OP-18 — Labour & team credit (resolution note)

> **Status:** ✅ **CLOSED 2026-08-05 as the C3 blocker.** Foundations → v0.6 (§1.1, §3.4a, §10, §12); Objections → v0.7 (B9, status board, §0). Opened and closed same day.

(α) labour-across-co-products = **declared convention: labour rides the process's material split**; co-product cost is **embodied input, never scarcity** (Method 2 yield-weighting rejected as price-rationing). ## 1. The opening finding — OP-18 is two problems, not one

The register (`Objections v0.6` A1) states OP-18 as "responsibility for joint work is not divisible … Aequitas needs [the welder-caused-40%-of-the-bridge number] to credit nine people separately." **Splitting that apart:**

| | Sub-problem | Genuine? |
|---|---|---|
| **(α)** | **Labour-hours across CO-PRODUCTS.** One labour process → several products (farmer's 8 hrs → beef + hide + bone). C3 needs "labour embodied in the hide"; the hour left no trace to a product. | ✅ **Genuine. This is the C3 blocker.** |
| **(β)** | **Credit across TEAM MEMBERS.** N people → one output (9 build a bridge). "How much credit each?" | ⚠️ **Largely dissolves — see §2.** |

## 2. (β) dissolves against A2 — check the axiom before importing a solution

Under A2 / §6: **one credit = time worked; everyone earns at the same rate.** So each team member is credited for **the hours they personally worked** — 8 for the farmer, 6 for the welder. **No output-decomposition is required to credit a team.** The "welder caused 40% of the bridge" number the register says Aequitas *needs* — **it does not need.** Credit is not a share of output; it is hours worked. The register conflated *credit-for-hours* with *share-of-responsibility*.

This is the CLAUDE.md pattern again: *the answer was already implied by an axiom nobody had read closely enough.* (Same shape as A2 resolving OP-17, A3 resolving the circulation-failure class.)

**Residue of (β), still genuine but narrow:** apportioning a *jointly-caused debit* (pollution/harm from a team process) across the team. This is a debit-attribution convention, not a credit one, and it is minor — a Level-2 responsibility question, sibling to OP-25. Park it; it does not block C3.

## 3. The real problem is (α), and it is a DEBIT-side pricing convention — it changes no one's credit

Key clarification that lowers the stakes: the farmer's 8 hours are **credited to the farmer, undivided,** no matter what. (α) only decides how those 8 hours read as **debit-cost split across the co-products** — i.e. the hide's price vs the beef's price. **No human's credit balance depends on the (α) convention.** It is a cost-comparison number, and therefore falls under the same rival-sector-audit discipline as any other cost constant (§3.3a), not under the credit rules.

So (α) is: *given a labour process with no per-product trace, how do we state each co-product's labour-cost?* Per the physical-trace test, **no trace → declare a convention.** The remaining work is to choose and justify it (or dissolve it).

## 4. The resolution — labour rides the material split (author's decision, 2026-08-05)

**Dissolve was rejected by the author:** co-products **must** carry labour, alongside material and energy. (They do *not* carry capital — that rides the team + pledgers, OP-23 — nor pollution — that stays with the causing team, §3.2b.) So (α) is **convention-first**, and the convention is:

> **Labour rides the process's own material split** — the same θ §3.4a measures (mass/deposition for cattle, cracking-energy for a refinery). No new basis, no new lever: it inherits the rival-audited material split. It changes **no one's credit** (the worker earns their own hours regardless); it only sets each co-product's *debit-cost*.

**Axiom score:** efficiency ✅, symmetry ✅, **dummy ✅** (manure ≈ 0 mass → ≈ 0 labour), additivity ✅. **Exploit:** amplifies the reward for faking the material split — but that is the existing §3.3a rival-audit target, no new mechanism. **Universality:** two identical cows in two towns get the same split (nothing demand-contingent) — the advantage over the rejected Method 2.

### 4a. The load-bearing sub-decision — cost ≠ scarcity (the tenderloin case)

The author raised, and then rejected, a rival: **Method 2 — yield-weighting**, where a rare cut (tenderloin, ≈1% yield) costs ~5× a common one (hamburger, ≈5% yield) per pound, "to capture scarcity."

**Rejected because scarcity is value, not cost.** A pound of each embodied the same feed, water, and growing-labour, so they *cost* the same (refined only by measured tissue composition — lean vs fat differ in deposition energy — never by desirability). Two decisive problems:
1. **It re-imports price-rationing.** If tenderloin costs 5×, it flows to whoever can absorb the larger debit — rationing by economic standing, the exact mechanism A5/§7.1 removes.
2. **It manufactures a capture surface.** It needs sector-set scarcity-weights per cut; whoever sets them steers every price. Method 1 needs a standards body only to *define cut boundaries*, then weighs this cow's actual cuts.

**Where the scarcity correctly goes** (the author's own "this is where the free market exists"): the **demand side** — pledges/signals set how many cattle are raised — and **decentralised local distribution** — the butcher's lottery, queue, or pledge-priority decides who gets the scarce cut. Irreducible scarcity (only ~10 lb of tenderloin per cow) is handled by a *distribution rule*, never by inflating cost. **USDA-as-service** (a standards body transformed from regulator to credited, trust-network-audited service) defines the cut categories — compatible with Method 1 and lighter than Method 2 would need.

## 5. Residue (non-blocking)

Apportioning a **jointly-caused debit** (team pollution or later-discovered harm) across the members who caused it. A debit-attribution convention, minor, sibling to OP-25. Parked — does not block C3.
