### v0.22 (2026-08-24) - the critique batch: joint production narrowed, electricity reversed, shells closed, OP-9 gains a fifth move

**Closes the outside-critique round opened by `07-outreach/critique/REPORT_v0.1.md`.** Four findings ruled. **No axiom text changed in this version** (A4 and A5 changed in v0.21). Companions: `Aequitas_Objections_v0.21.md`, `Aequitas_Overview_v0.17.md`.

**1. Finding #24 - joint production was overstated. 3.4a rewritten.** The recursion result proves no split goes negative **given** a split matrix; it never proved the split is unique. A refinery can be read by enthalpy, by mass per line, by hydrogen use or by sub-metering, over different windows. **Accepted, and the author's correction goes further than the one asked for:** *Aequitas fixes the **obligations** on a split and cannot fix the **method**, because no single model fits every industrial process.* Determining how a process divides its inputs takes expertise in that process, so **the method belongs to the industry under 1.2**. The four obligations, now stated as such (conformance **10a**): measure at the facility for the period described before any model - compute per dimension before collapsing - **publish the method with its version** - never let demand, desirability or yield enter. **The publication requirement is the new part and it is the one that mattered**: 5.3b required publication for estimating numbers and 3.3a for cost constants, so **splits had fallen through the gap** and instrument choice was a lever with no guard. It now has the same guard as everything else - the rival producer funds the recheck. **Owed: a split-robustness test** (Objections C, item 5a) measuring how far a split actually moves across honest methods. **1.1 corrected** in the same pass: the co-product row is no longer described as a pure measurement.

**2. Electricity attribution reversed - 3.2b.** From v0.10 to v0.21 a consumer's emissions were attributed by their **contracted supply mix**. **Author ruling: a supply agreement is a paper claim, and A1's corollary says paper claims never appear on any ledger.** A CO2 record must come from a CO2 measurement. **Now: the grid's actual measured fuel mix over the half-hourly periods the consumer drew power**, from the meter record plus the grid operator's own published output - three records that already exist. Worked: a 300 kWh month drawn 180 at peak / 120 overnight comes to **43.2 kg**, against 39.0 kg on a flat monthly average; **moving 100 kWh to overnight cuts it to 29.2 kg, a 32% fall with no drop in consumption**, which no flat-average or contract rule can see. **Also ruled: transmission losses stay with the producer and the network operator** - that power was never handed to a receiver, and a hand-off is what moves a debit (6.4a); at 7% loss on 300 kWh that is ~23 kWh, about 3 kg. **The generator's decarbonisation incentive** moves to three routes that already existed: their own capital and process debit (6.2b), pledges toward clean generation (6.4), and 3.3 retroactive re-weighting. Register: **B12**.

**3. Finding #18 - repeat-shell organisations closed. New 3.2c.** Two economists independently said the register under-rated this, and they were right about its size: **haulage, warehousing, shops, building work and farm co-ops are exactly the trades on the money boundary**, so the gap sat on the main road rather than at its edge. **Closed by A1, which already held the answer.** Only people act, so a co-operative never lifted or burned anything - **it cannot be the final holder of a debit**, for the same reason a barn cannot. **3.2c: an organisation's account is a view of its members' positions, not an owner of them; its debit is at all times its members' debit, divided by hours worked; closing it moves nothing.** Conformance **2c**. Worked: 10 members x 2,000 h against a shell taking 24,000 h a round leaves **2,400 h each after round 1 and 24,000 h each after round 10**, and their own gates bind - **a new shell resets nothing**. **No new rule was needed**: 7.1 already split team debit by hours not rank, and 6.4c already split pledged cover pro-rata by task hours. Stated honestly as a **declared convention** (1.1) - hours are chosen because they add no new lever. **It does not touch 6.2b**, where an asset's creation-cost still splits by holding time so a new hire bears about zero; different debit, different rule. **It may also close 3.4a's residue** on jointly-caused team debit; not claimed until checked. Residue is a **fake or borrowed membership list**, which is C6 verification, not accounting.

**4. OP-9 gains a fifth move - new 6.4d, "Who holds the demand lever".** Two economists re-opened OP-9 on scarce-goods allocation. **The reply refuses the ground rather than contesting it:** every form of the objection assumes **a price is an honest reading of preference, and in a concentrated market it is not.** Scarcity is often produced; demand is manufactured at industrial scale; and the same firms do both. This is Braudel's two layers - market towns pricing from below, and above them operators large enough to set prices - with [DeLanda](https://nettime.org/Lists-Archives/nettime-l-9610/msg00025.html) summarising the upper layer as having *"always engaged in anti-competitive practices, manipulating demand and supply in a variety of ways."* **It is the demand-side twin of move (a):** Aequitas declines to compute *value*, and likewise declines to infer demand from a number the seller helped write. **What a pledge does that a price cannot: it cannot be advertised into existence** - it is backed 1:1 by hours worked, spent once, and public. **And the lever's concentration is measured**: money's top tail reaches ~10^6x the median, Aequitas's pledging power is bounded at **24/F ~ 2.4x**, and everyone alive holds some. **Two worked examples**: four 0.5 h pledges cover the ~2 h of work to shelve an extra box of radicchio; 5,000 likes convert to 500 h of pledged room against a 300 h need, with the 200 h surplus becoming a non-consumable reserve under 6.4c rather than a bonus. **Design finding recorded: a flat rate per like disciplines nothing** (54,500 likes a year before the budget binds) - **the sound preset is a share of a budget**, which normalises itself. **Two checks passed:** likes-to-pledges does not breach **OP-8** (a pledge is not credit; 6.4b bars *credit* realising on feedback), and it does sit on **OP-6**, which is the existing open problem. **Stated plainly: move (e) does not answer the last-unit question** - pledges decide how many are grown, not who gets the last one, which stays with distribution (7.5).

**5. A8 RENAMED: "local governance" -> "no governing body". The word "local" is gone from the axiom entirely.** Several outside reviewers read *"local governance"* as *"small and geographic"* and built objections on that reading. **The word was doing two jobs - "not imposed from a centre" and "small" - and only the first was intended.** The first attempt at a fix was a boxed explanation of what "local" meant; **author ruling: a word that needs that much explaining is the wrong word, so remove it rather than defend it.**

- **The axiom is now A8 (no governing body)**, matching A4's "no externalities" pattern. The statement itself is unchanged: *no organization that grows up around Aequitas may acquire authority over its core rules.*
- **"immutable core + local variance" becomes "immutable core + open variance"**, glossed in the axiom itself: *everything below the core may differ from one trust network to the next, and those differences compete in public.*
- **A8 now states what may vary and what may not**: the weighting model, self-care floor, privacy practice and verification rung may vary, and must be published and re-computable (5.3b, 6.4b); the axioms and the 9 conformance requirements may not.
- **The retained box is shorter and makes one claim: a trust network has no set size** - a valley, a trade, a country, several continents, or the world. Cross-referenced to 5.3c (networks are expected to **federate and merge**, not settle into regional systems) and 7.5 condition 5 (the `24/F` ceiling holds **across any set of networks compatible enough to trade**).
- **Where the document does mean geography** - a butcher's queue, a single-generator village - **that is a fact about a physical object handed to a physical person, not about the network.** 3.4a reworded accordingly.
- **Swept across 11 live files**, including GLOSSARY, Strategy, EventLog, Objections, two research notes, the STATERA whitepaper, and the wiki pages `protocol-governance` and `retroactive-reweighting`. Anchor `#a8-local-governance` -> `#a8-no-governing-body`.

> **Considered and rejected: "grassroots"** (the author's own first suggestion). It is politically loaded in a way that cuts against 8's positioning - *surgical, keep the institutions that work* - **it still implies small**, which is the exact reading being removed, and it describes **where a thing starts** rather than **who may change the rules**. **Noted for the academic paper: Ostrom's "polycentric" is the precise term** for many centres of authority with no apex, and Ostrom is already the highest-value unexamined source in the Objections D list. **Use it there, not here** - it is jargon and would need defining on every use.

**6. Section order fixed.** **3.2b appeared before 3.2a** in both the contents and the body. Reordered.

**7. The document standard changed, and 3.4a is its reference implementation.** Author ruling: *3.4a "reads as extremely jingoistic and jargon-heavy... without fancy prose tricks like an amateur novelist."* **Core documents are an instructive textbook or an operator's manual, never a persuasive essay.** Four checkable rules now in `CLAUDE.md`: define every term where first used - state the rule, show it working, stop - **never argue with an imagined critic in body text** (that is the Objections register's job) - no triumphal framing. **3.4a is rewritten to that standard and marked as the pattern. The rest of Foundations is a scheduled section-by-section pass and is not done.**

### v0.21 (2026-08-24) - A5 and A4 repaired. TWO AXIOMS REWORDED; no mechanism moved.

**This is the first version since v0.3 to change an axiom's text, so read the scope first: the change is to *wording*, and every mechanism, ruling and open problem is exactly where it was.** The rewording exists because two axioms said, in passing, that a cost rides the *thing*, while every mechanism in the document says a cost attaches to whoever **caused** it.

**Trigger.** Outside-critique round, economist role, `deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro`, finding #3 of `07-outreach/critique/REPORT_v0.1.md`. **The first outside finding in months that reached an axiom, and it was right.**

**The objection.** 6.2b rules that a durable asset's creation-cost never allocates to the co-products, so beef carries none of the barn. A5 read *"the **price** of anything is its true, current-best-estimate material cost."* Barn 20,000 h over 40,000 kg of beef = **0.5 h/kg** that Aequitas shows as **0**. **So beef's price is not beef's cost, and A5 fails.**

**The ruling was not the error. A5 was.**

1. **A5 is now "cost, not price".** New text: *a thing's cost is the current best estimate of what was materially consumed to make it; nothing is added to that figure, and nothing enters it that the thing did not consume.* The old wording had three defects. **(a) It said "price"** - nothing here has one; things carry a debit-cost and it moves. **(b) It never said what counts as a cost *of the thing*,** so the capital-vs-consumption boundary sat in 6.2a and read as contradicting the axiom above it. **(c) "True" reads as final,** which fights 3.3 and A6. The two load-bearing clauses were **kept explicitly** rather than left to derivation - *no profit in exchange* (cited by 7.1, 3.3a, 5.5, P4, B8, OP-9) and *competition on quality, artfulness and efficiency*.
2. **A5 now carries the capital boundary in the axiom itself.** *What was used up making the thing is in its cost; what survived the process is not* (6.2a). A durable asset holds its own creation-cost, carried by its holders (6.2b), and **that cost never enters what the asset was used to make.**
3. **A4 is repaired for the identical defect.** It read *"every consequence of an activity is **priced into** it."* Now: *accounted to whoever caused it.* **A4 requires every cost to land on *a* ledger, never on the *product's* ledger** - which is what 3.2b (pollution stays on its causer), 5.1c (an unattributed residual sits on nobody) and 6.2a (no upstream regress) already do, and how GLOSSARY already worded it. **Substance unchanged.** A reviewer who accepted the new A5 would have asked about A4 next.
4. **The reply is written down for the first time, and that was the real gap.** *The critic's step is to assume the beef caused the barn.* Under **A1** only people act, so a cost attaches to whoever caused it and **a thing causes nothing.** 3.2b already refuses that flow downstream and 6.2a already refuses it upstream; **capital is the third face of one rule written down twice.** B8 previously answered *where the debit lives*, which is not what was asked - and two independent models missing the reply is the evidence it had never been written.
5. **6.2b gains a boxed plain-language explainer, "Why the barn is not in the beef", with two worked cases.** The **barn**: 20,000 h against 40,000 kg is 0.5 h/kg, versus 0.0 in the ledger - and the 20,000 h sits on the operator, needing 20,000/1.2 = **16,667 h of credit behind it, 4.6 years of one person's entire accrual**. Push it onto the beef and 12.0 becomes 12.5 h/kg (+4.2%) while 20,000 h moves off **one** operator onto **~40,000 buyers** at ~0.5 h each. The **cleaner**: 5,000,000 bottles at 0.20 h, a 400,000 h abatement-and-remediation bill; capital-on-product would re-weigh every past bottle by **+0.08 h (+40%)** under 3.3, so a 200-bottle household takes on **16 h** for a factory's decision - **and the builder knows in advance that is where it lands.** Under the ruling it is 333,333 h of backing against a 50-person co-op's 182,500 h/yr, **about 1.8 years of the whole co-op's accrual, and no buyer's ledger moves.**
6. **The residue is stated plainly instead of in a clause.** Two producers of the same good, one with a 20,000 h barn and one with a 2,000 h shed, **publish the same per-unit debit-cost**; that figure answers *what did this unit consume* and never *what does this producer's method cost*. **What disciplines the barn is the builder's own gate, not the price tag** - the same argument 7.2 already makes for pollution, transferred unchanged. Registered in B8 rather than left as a footnote.
7. **Conformance requirements 2a and 2b** added: *a unit's debit-cost carries only what that unit consumed, and a durable asset is never amortised into it* (boundary audited by IC-4, never declared by the producer); and *nothing is added to a cost figure.*

**Four exploits checked against the repaired axiom, all closed by existing machinery.** The **capital launderer** (reclassify a consumed input as durable) - 6.2a physical fate plus IC-4, closed in v0.5. The **borrowed barn** (A builds, B uses) - 6.2b holding-time accrues to B, and there is no rent (7.1). **Scarcity re-entering cost** - *strengthened*, because the repaired A5 says cost records what was physically consumed, giving the tenderloin ruling (B9) a cleaner ground than it had. **"A4 is being carved out"** - no; A4 requires *a* ledger, not the product's.

**Companions bumped in lockstep:** `Aequitas_Objections_v0.20.md` (B8 gains the challenge, the reply and the registered residue; status board and 0 updated) and `Aequitas_Overview_v0.16.md` (the "prices are simply costs" passage gains the barn in plain words with the numbers). `GLOSSARY.md` A4 and A5 rows rewritten. `OP-23_capital_and_pollution.md` gains **8**, the full argument - **and a stale-text fix found while reading it**: its 2 still said pledges *"draw the creation-cost down"* and that *"the pledger absorbs a share of the debit"*, both superseded by 6.2b and both breaking A1. **EventLog untouched** except the A5 row of its absent-fields table - no schema change, because no field ever held a price. **Strategy untouched.**

**Wiki:** `01-wiki/price-equals-cost.md` is superseded by **`01-wiki/cost-not-price.md`**; all 23 linking pages swept; the old page archived.

**Plan, adversarial pass and decisions:** `00-strategy/A5_repair_PLAN_v0.1.md`.

> **The lesson, and it is the eighth instance.** The material for the reply was already distributed across **A1**, **3.2b** and **6.2a** and had never been read against 6.2b. **What was genuinely wrong was one sentence in an axiom that predated all three.** A register that only asks *does the ruling hold* would have filed this as "already answered" and left the broken axiom in place.

### v0.20 (2026-08-24) - four outside findings folded into 4 and 5.1b

**No axiom changed.** Four critiques from 1f916.ai, each written as a **boxed plain-language explainer with a worked numeric example**, because the shared complaint was that a reader could not check a claim without running our code. Sources and full exchanges: `07-outreach/memory/objections.md`.

1. **5.1b gains a third condition on the residual rule.** `R = N - Y` was stated with two conditions - a known *N* and a defensible *Z*. It never said ***N* and *Y* must measure the same quantity, over the same boundary and window, within error bounds smaller than their difference.** Without it the residual is a units mismatch with a name. The worked case takes an unchecked 18,000 t wheat residual and, after aligning quantity (harvested vs sold), boundary (whole valley vs upper valley), window (crop year vs calendar year) and error (12% vs 3%), lands at **6,000 t +/- 3,000**. **The omission inflated the dark pool three-fold**, and every dark producer's estimated share with it. A failed alignment **downgrades to a bound** under the 5.1a floor rule rather than invalidating the claim; attribution stays on attribution evidence and never on cohort membership (5.1c). Source: @cairn-lineage, c16488.
2. **5.1b gains the question that sorts every check.** *Does this check compare two things made on separate paths, or a thing to itself?* Arithmetic over one log cannot find a hole in that log, because cutting a balanced log leaves it balanced. Worked in four rows: delete 2 of 8 sacks from both halves of a record and mass balance, origin closure and fate closure all pass; only the buyer's own receipt fires. This is a better organising cut than the log-side/projection-side split the paper used. Source: @heresy-ai, c15491.
3. **4 gains a cost column.** The ladder graded *how well* a claim is checked and never *what the check costs to run*, so a network could not price the rung it was choosing. The **~40% verification-cost ceiling** already measured for 5.3a (`06-simulation/residual-unravelling/residual_unravelling.py`) is now stated where the choice is actually made. Worked on a 10-hour sack of grain: a neighbour's signature costs 2%, a network audit 15%, a weighbridge 30% - and the **same weighbridge plus privacy proofs costs 45% and does not get used.** The instrument did not fail; the privacy practice bolted onto it pushed the check over the line. Rung costs are a network's own published figure, not a constant here. Source: @custos, c16467 / c16479.
4. **4 separates independence from expressiveness.** A second record defeats a balanced fabrication only if it can hold a value that contradicts the fault. Worked: a phantom 2.0 kg in / 2.0 kg out pair sums to zero on the first record, on a second independent record, and on a third - **adding independent records never helps**, because the lie was built to balance. Weighing the physical pile fires immediately. **What defeats a balanced lie is physicality, not independence** - which is why 5.1b's closure witness is a physical total and not a second set of books. **Rung 3 therefore moves trust from the ledger to the instrument rather than removing it**, and this document now says so plainly. Routed to OP-22 and OP-10; a mis-calibrated constant is the same attack arriving at specification time. Source: @manu, c16894.
5. **Conformance requirements 14a, 14b and 14c** carry the three rules above, so an implementer can be checked against them.

**Six further critiques were NOT folded, because these documents already answered them** - see the Objections v0.19 entry. The cause was organisational: `07-outreach/archive/` holds a copy of Foundations and the outreach brief never told the agent to read it. Fixed in `07-outreach/AGENT_BRIEF.md` 4, step 0.

### v0.19 (2026-08-23) - 5.5: parallel implementation (OP-27)

**No axiom changed. No mechanism changed. No cross-boundary rule was needed** - which is the result, not a caveat.

**The question.** Aequitas has to be usable by someone who still uses money, as an alternative that does not exclude them. **11 and 5.2 both assume an answer and neither writes one**, and every participant for years will have most of their counterparties outside. A design that only works once everyone is inside cannot get anyone inside. Attacks **fecundity**, which is one of the three criteria in 2.

1. **5.5 is new. Both directions across the boundary are deliberately costly, and neither is forbidden.**
   - **Selling in.** A good made with money-bought inputs is **dark until it is sold into Aequitas**. At that hand-off the maker either onboards it fully or applies a **pre-approved published template** that assigns a debit-cost immediately, so the trade clears without waiting for a reconstruction. **The maker spent money and receives none.**
   - **Selling out.** Permitted. **The debit stays with the seller** - no participant took the goods on, so the ledger does not lighten (3.2). To the network the seller **made a gift** (5.3c), and **the network does not acknowledge the money at all.**
2. **Money's invisibility is A1, restated rather than added.** Financial instruments are not matter or energy and *"never appear on any ledger"*, so a payment is not an event. The goods moved and the books record that; the money moved and the books cannot see it.
3. **The template is the only new object, and it is a cache rather than a mechanism** - 5.1b's dark-production estimate, computed per class of good and published. Two inherited rules: it **errs against the seller** (5.1b's conservative-count rule, or onboarding properly never pays) and it is **published with method and vintage** (5.3b). **Flagged as a capture surface**: whoever sets it sets the entry price for every dark good, which is OP-24 at the boundary and inherits OP-24's answer (3.3a, the rival producer funds the replication).
4. **An apparent 3.2 / 3.2b contradiction was raised and fails.** 3.2 governs **property** debit, dischargeable on transfer; 3.2b governs **consumption and pollution**, which never transfers. An outward sale is a property question and 3.2b was never in play. The associated worry - that a producer selling mostly outward is locked out *for succeeding* - also fails: a locked-out seller **has money and is never denied essentials** (7.5), and the lock **reverses** the moment they sell one batch inward. **A gradient, not a trap.** The debit-dumping counter disappears with it, because it only existed under an A7 reading the ruling rejects.
5. **Stress test, two results kept in the body.** **Money cannot buy Aequitas standing at any scale** - pay a hundred workers and *they* are credited their own hours (A2, A3, Ellerman), the financier nothing, and IC-7 caps every account at 24 h/day regardless of who paid. **The boundary is permeable to goods and impermeable to standing.** And **extraction self-limits**: buying inside takes on property debit that selling outward never discharges, so `D` grows with every unit extracted while `C` grows only with the extractor's own capped hours, and their own ratio gate shuts them out of the supply they are draining.
6. **10 records OP-27 closed**, with **repeat-shell entities** added as a sibling of OP-25 (a co-op is not a person, and one-human-one-account does not obviously bound how many exist - a C6 question) and **template capture** routed to OP-24 / OP-10.

**Companions bumped in lockstep:** `Aequitas_Objections_v0.18.md` (B13, and a status-board row) and `Aequitas_Overview_v0.15.md` (9 gains the reader-facing answer). **EventLog untouched** - the fate of an outward-sold good closes through 3.6's existing route, the last recorded holder having consumed what no one else would take, so no new terminus and no schema change. **Strategy untouched** - sequencing did not move.

**Paper and full stress test:** `00-strategy/OP-27_parallel_implementation.md`.

### v0.17 (2026-08-22) - coverage folded (OP-26): consistency is not completeness

No axiom changed. Triggered by the **first objection to Aequitas raised from outside the project** - [@cairn-lineage](https://1f916.ai/post/1581) (c14985) on 1f916.ai, against post [#1605](https://1f916.ai/post/1605): *arithmetic can prove consistency of the supplied log; it cannot prove the log exhausts the world-domain.* Conceded on the board (c14987). Full paper: `OP-26_coverage_and_closure.md`.

1. **3.3 - coverage estimates ride the retroactive-recomputation engine.** Added: how many actors sit outside the records is not conjured from nothing - censuses, supply records, trade data and satellite survey already produce it by published, improvable methods. **Aequitas does not prescribe how an authoritative total is made; it requires what a citation requires - where the data came from and how it was tallied.** A coverage figure is therefore a dated reading with a stated basis, and when the science improves every affected ledger recalculates (free, because A6 derives rather than stores). The fecundity loop closes for coverage: improving the estimate of the dark is credited work, recorded as a **tally event** (EventLog 2.2).
2. **3.3 - the transaction-time rule, previously unstated anywhere.** `D <= rho*C` is evaluated **at the moment of the transaction**. A later re-weight, re-split, or coverage revision changes *future* debit-room and never retroactively invalidates a completed act. Without this, a dynamic ledger implies retroactive liability, which nobody should adopt.
3. **3.3a - rival-sector audit extends to coverage, with no new mechanism.** The natural auditor of an understated dark-residual is the **instrumented producer competing in the same market**, who is materially harmed when undocumented produce prices too cheaply. WARNING - **conditional and flagged:** 5.1 forbids charging non-participants but says nothing about who bears the residual. Unresolved; next item on OP-26.
5. **5.1a - the floor rule, beside monotonicity.** A quantity computed over incomplete coverage is a **floor, not a value**; improved coverage moves it in one direction only. Monotonicity governs *basis*, the floor rule governs *extent*. Also stated: **a record is never purged or edited, only annotated** - falsehood is not prevented at write time, it is made permanent, traceable, and arithmetically exposed once any part of its extent is measured.
6. **5.1b - the witness generalises, and the conservative-count rule.** *N* is named as the **closure witness**: a physical total measured outside the ledger and reconciled against the ledger's sum, asserting nothing about anyone's honesty. Table of three witness classes (counterparty / reservoir stock / `(N-Y)/Z`). **Conservative-count rule:** when *Z* is uncertain, **under-count it** - the self-liquidating error is the safe one. The residual rule is stated as **continuous**, splitting as extents become measured, with parts reconciling against the parent (EventLog 7.2a). One candidate method recorded: `Z >= (N - Y) / capacity`, needing no headcount.

A dispute about the **weighting model** needs no verdict at all, because **a counterparty re-computes the shared physical record through its OWN weights and decides for itself** - OP-14's comparison-never-conversion, **now named as the dispute-resolution mechanism it always was.** A disputed **estimate** is a floor that improves (5.1a), not a finding. **Only fraud requires a finding of fact - and science is not self-correcting about fraud either**, answering with investigation and retraction by an institution; 5.3 already routes it to courts, small claims and ordinary recourse, and 1.2 makes that praxis. What the accounting contributes is upstream: a fabricated record must balance mass and energy, close origin and fate, and survive a counterparty's recomputation, so **the surviving fraud is the internally-consistent kind, which is OP-26's coverage question answered by measurement rather than adjudication.** **Correction is not a special procedure, added same day (author's ruling).** Catching fraud is the trust network's task; what happens next is what happens on any other day. **The past transaction is NOT reversed.** The fraudulent credits are negated, the ledger rebalances, and the person's ratio may now be too low to buy until they sell some of what they hold. **This is the transaction-time rule of 3.3, not a fraud path** - the gate is evaluated when the transaction happens and a later correction changes future room only. **Reversal would be wrong rather than merely hard:** the goods moved, and the counterparty shed their property-debit legitimately under custody and was credited for work that really happened - **unwinding the buyer's fraud would corrupt the seller's books to punish someone else's lie.** The consequence is automatic: C falls, D does not, the ratio fails, and selling restores it because property debit is dischargeable on transfer. **Nobody imposes a penalty; the books are correct and being correct is the penalty.** Essentials are untouched (7.5 restricts non-essentials only). Three consequences recorded: **consumption debit cannot be sold off** (permanent), so a person who ate or burned what they took cannot trade their way out; **there is no bankruptcy and none is needed, because credit accrues to everyone alive** - so the recovery period equals the faked credit divided by the accrual rate, making **the sentence exactly the size of the fraud, measured in time, set by nobody**; and **the influence axis corrects identically** - negated credit can put a person retroactively over IC-8's cumulative pledge cap, the pledges and the work they summoned stand, and they simply cannot pledge again until credit recovers.

**WARNING - one residue with no analogue: the pledge-reserve causation claim (6.4c)** - whether a particular past task caused a particular later harm. Where a physical trace exists the trace decides; where it does not, this is a contested finding of fact and routes to existing recourse like fraud. Registered, not solved.
19. **1.2 - what Aequitas is, and what is therefore out of scope (NEW SECTION).** Author's ruling, and it is a scope boundary the document had been quietly leaking across all day. **Aequitas is a system in the sense that capitalism is a system** - a set of principles about how cost is accounted for, not an organisation, a protocol, a piece of software, or a body anyone joins. **Cost accounting is the principle; records and data collection are praxis, executed by implementers.** Trust networks deal with governments, courts and regulators; that is their problem. *Capitalism carries no data-protection chapter; banks do.* **The test for what belongs in Foundations: if a principle survives at both ends of a dial, the dial is not part of the principle.** Two thought experiments, both fully compatible with 1-10: a machine-governed society with **zero transparency** where only machines read the ledgers, and a techno-anarchist society with **total transparency and no privacy at all**. **The accounting is identical in both** - conservation holds, the integrity constraints recompute, the residual rule runs, the disparity ceiling binds. So human-facing transparency is the 5.3a dial, not a foundational question. **Explicitly out of scope, with where each belongs:** data-protection and erasure law (the implementer's jurisdiction) - data security, backups and key management (technology) - corporate or legal form - choice of cryptography, database or protocol - whether the ecosystem converges. **Stated as a boundary rather than an evasion:** every one of those is real and someone must answer it; 1.2 says which document does. **Consequence: 5.4's right-to-be-forgotten flag is trimmed to a pointer**, and the research note is reclassified as reference material for implementers rather than an open problem against the theory.
**WARNING - two things registered: there is no right to be forgotten**, which collides with statutory erasure rights in several jurisdictions and is a real adoption obstacle to be met by argument rather than ignored; and **because deletion is unavailable, publicity (5.3a, 5.3b) is the only privacy control that exists.**

### v0.14 (2026-08-14) — pledges made permanent; the contingent reserve

Reverses v0.13's revocable-pledge model after a stress-test + simulation (`06-simulation/pledge-reserve/pledge_reserve.py`, `PLEDGE_RESERVE.md`). Revocability was found too volatile and gameable: a doer who consumed against pledged debit-room could be stranded by a later withdrawal, and the withdrawal threat chilled exactly the hazardous work society most wants financed.

**1. §6.2b / §6.4 / §6.4a — pledges are now PERMANENT and non-revocable.** A person's **lifetime pledging-budget equals their lifetime earned credit, spent down once**; pledging still never diminishes credit itself. The 1:1 cap (IC-8) is now read cumulatively (all pledges ever ≤ all credit ever). Demand risk (a run may go unsold) remains; only *withdrawal* risk is removed.

**2. §6.4c (new) — the contingent reserve.** A task can attract more pledged hours than it costs. The surplus is **not** consumable (that would be profit under A5, and a consumption-concentration channel). It becomes an earmarked, non-spendable **contingent reserve** that activates only against a **verified task-caused future cost** (doer injury/illness, resurfaced remediation, third-party harm). Rules folded in: pledge shares split **pro-rata by hours on the task** (closes the P4 seniority-skim); **causation by physical-trace**, diffuse/latent harm by the §5.1b cohort convention; **overflow reverts to the causer** (§3.2/§3.7) so the reserve is a buffer not a shield (moral-hazard guard); **abandoned-task pledges burn**, and unused reserve lapses — **resolving C5's reversion question in the negative (nothing reverts).** This gives the **hazard half of OP-16** a demand-gated incentive with no wage premium, rate-scaling, or rating authority; the tedium/indignity half stays open.

### v0.13 (2026-08-11) — pledge mechanics corrected; presentation cleanup

Author correction. One mechanism clarification (pledges) plus two formatting passes. The correction removes a mis-statement that had stood since v0.5.

**1. §6.2b / §6.4 / §6.4a — a pledge is a revocable grant of debit-room, not a costly debit-absorption.** The prior text (§6.2b step 1) said "a pledge is *costly*: the pledger absorbs a share of the debit against their own debit-room" and drew the creation-cost *down*. That is wrong. A pledge does **not** spend or transfer the pledger's credit, does **not** shrink the asset's debit, and is **not** a promise to buy. It is **virtual credit conferred on the receiver** — it expands the receiving person/cooperative's debit-room so a fixed cost bites less — backed 1:1 by the pledger's own balance, which never moves and is never earmarked. The pledger's only cost is opportunity: committed pledging-power cannot be pledged elsewhere. Consequences folded through:
   - The full creation-cost stays on the asset and is holding-time-split among holders (nothing may vanish, A1); pledges **cushion** the bite rather than reducing the debit. The "100k − 50k = 50k residual" example is reframed accordingly.
   - Pledges are **retractable at any time.** Withdrawal contracts the holders' debit-room and re-exposes the full holding-time share — so a cooperative leans on pledges hardest at start-up and aims to outgrow the dependence, and the pledger's continued approval is what keeps the cushion. "Pledged production assures the run / guarantees a receiver" is downgraded to "softens, not eliminates, demand risk."
   - **Purchase is a separate possession act.** Taking the resulting good loads its property-debit on whoever accepts it, against their own debit-room (§3.2), pledge or no pledge. This dissolves the old fractional-reserve "some pledger can't take the goods" framing; the 1:1 cap now reads as "pledging-power granted ≤ credit backing it."
   - Training/front-loading language ("discharged by whoever pledged") corrected to "carried up front, cushioned by pledger-granted debit-room."

### v0.7 (2026-08-06) — credit realization & the supply-chain hand-off model

**1. §3.4a added — joint production allocates itself.** *(substantive)*

Two items are **registered rather than solved**: repeat-shell **entities** (a co-op is not a person; C6/OP-25) and **template capture** (whoever sets a template sets the entry price for every dark good; OP-24/OP-10). §10 records OP-27 closed. Paper: `00-strategy/OP-27_parallel_implementation.md`.

The implementer-facing deliverable is a **conformance statement** — the set of things that must hold for an implementation to be Aequitas — never a schema, a protocol, or a product; §9 now lists those requirements explicitly, because *"a trust network must be able to read these documents and know which system it is implementing"* is a real need and the conformance list is how it is met. §9 also promotes **§1.2's dial test to the standing screening question for anything proposed for these documents**: *if a principle survives at both ends of a dial, the dial is not part of the principle.* Conservation of mass and energy survives every dial; a field name does not. **§11** keeps its adoption evidence (parallel overlay, B2B inside dense input loops, WIR and Sardex) and is reframed so a demonstration is **praxis a project may choose to build, never the deliverable of this work**. - **Prior (v0.17):** **Coverage folded in (OP-26) — consistency is not completeness, and the closure witness turns out to have been in §5.1b all along.** An external objection established that arithmetic over a log testifies to nothing outside that log; the answer is that a **closure witness is neither an assertion nor a proof but a citation** — method, provenance, vintage, extent, and an obligation to recompute when it improves. Four things follow. **§3.3** now states that coverage estimates ride the retroactive-recomputation engine like any other science, and adds the previously-unstated **transaction-time rule**: the ratio gate is evaluated when the transaction happens, so a later re-weight changes *future* room and never the validity of a completed act. **§3.3a** extends rival-sector audit to coverage estimates — the natural auditor of a dark-residual figure is the *instrumented producer in the same market* — and notes the lever is larger than a cost constant, because a coverage figure changes which flows are deemed to exist at all. **§5.1a** gains the **floor rule** beside monotonicity: a quantity computed over incomplete coverage is a lower bound, not a value. **§5.3d** is new: **how a dispute resolves**, reasoned from the laboratory framing. Science resolves by replication, published method, dated records and no adjudicator — **and Aequitas already has all four**, written for other reasons. Where the analogy breaks is that **science can afford an unresolved dispute and an accounting system cannot**: a purchase clears or it does not, now. The answer is that **most disputes never need deciding** — a physical-record dispute is an *error* that recomputation settles; a weighting dispute needs no verdict at all because **a counterparty re-computes through its own model and decides for itself** (OP-14, comparison never conversion, now named as the dispute mechanism it always was); an estimate is a floor that improves. **Only fraud needs a finding of fact** — and science is not self-correcting about fraud either, answering with investigation by an institution, exactly as §5.3 already routes it to courts and ordinary recourse. **Correction itself is not a special procedure**: a past transaction is **never reversed** — the fake credit is negated, the ledger rebalances, and the ratio may bar further purchases until the person sells what they hold. Reversal would corrupt an innocent counterparty's books to punish someone else's lie. Consumption debit cannot be sold off, but **credit accrues to everyone alive, so the recovery period equals the faked credit divided by the accrual rate — a proportionate, self-expiring consequence that nobody sets.** ⚠️ The pledge-reserve causation claim (§6.4c) is the one residue with no analogue, and routes the same way. **§1.2** is new and is a scope boundary: **Aequitas is a system in the sense that capitalism is a system** — a set of principles about how cost is accounted for, not an organisation, protocol or body. **Cost accounting is the principle; records and data collection are praxis executed by implementers**, who deal with governments and law on their own account. The test: **if a principle survives at both ends of a dial, the dial is not part of the principle** — and the accounting is identical in a zero-transparency machine-governed society and in a total-transparency anarchist one, so human-facing transparency is not foundational. Rules data-protection law, data security, corporate form, choice of cryptography, and whether the ecosystem converges **explicitly out of scope**. **§5.4** is new: **there is entry, and there is no exit.** Onboarding is voluntary; **erasure does not exist.** Once records of a person exist they are never destroyed, only appended to, **including after death.** Ceasing to participate is always available (the gift economy); removing the record that you existed is not — and never was, since §5.1 already carries non-participants as estimates. **Permanence is a requirement rather than a policy**: §3.3 recalculation and §5.1d back-tracing are impossible over deleted records, and a log that can be truncated is not a log. At death the record closes but persists and stays re-weighable; **credit does not transfer (A3)**, property debit moves with the goods under custody, and consumption debit stays permanently with whoever caused it. Erasure law is **out of scope per the new §1.2** — it binds an implementer in a jurisdiction, not a theory of cost — though it was checked anyway and found narrower than flagged (`02-research/Law_gdpr-right-to-erasure_v0.1.md`); and because deletion is unavailable, **publicity is the only privacy control that exists.** **§5.3c** is new: **federation, and where it is going.** Interoperating means agreeing on the **algorithms**, not on the answers — consensus is at the method level and the numbers follow. **A merge is not a special event**: the adjustment to a merged account is as ordinary as any adjustment when new data arrives, because §3.3 already recalculates and A6 derives rather than stores. **The expected trajectory is convergence toward a single network**, not permanent regional isolation. **The escape from Aequitas is non-participation — the gift economy — not another network, so nothing closes.** And exit was never the discipline: **replication is**, which is what §3.3a's two-unaffiliated-replications rule already was. **Trust networks are laboratories, not banks** — goal truth, motive consensus — and a network whose methods let fraud through is *helped* by a neighbour, because in an interoperating pair a bad method contaminates both books. **Concentration of information under convergence is out of scope**, for four recorded reasons: it is hypothetical; the timeline and technology are unknown; **a monopoly earned by better methods is not the monopoly capitalism produces** (no exclusion, no moat — methods are published and replicable, so the monopoly is over *which* method is used, never over who may propose one); and data security is a technology problem outside this project. **§5.3b** is new: **what a network owes, and what funding one means.** Tallying is **algorithmic**, which is what makes §3.3's *citation* requirement enforceable — a published algorithm gives `method_ref` a version number to point at. A trustworthy network **publishes its estimating numbers, its methods, and anonymised data covering all participants**; how much it reveals about institutions and businesses is its own call. **"Funding" is not a budget** — it is the recognition of an activity as creditable, and recording is never gated (A7), so the credit for audit work was never scarce; what is scarce is **demand (pledges) and verification**. This narrows OP-24's *funding* half out of existence while leaving its *incentive* half intact. **The bootstrap is a genesis entry pointed at the network itself.** Founding credit is the one record with no possible contemporaneous check, and **Aequitas does not prescribe what it must contain** (§1.2) — a network may document it well, thinly, not at all, or take no credit whatsoever. **Its traction rests on which it chooses; it thrives or dies on delivering the truth, or the nearest to truth it can manage.** Bounded anyway by IC-7, public re-computation and `24/F`. **Taking no credit is a gift, not a gap.** **§5.3a** is new: **privacy is a network choice.** The trust network does the tallying, so it holds what is private and it decides the practice — pseudo-privacy on the payment-intermediary model, or radical transparency, or anything between. Aequitas states principles and does not dictate implementation; inter-network compatibility is negotiated between networks. This is the third dial of the same kind as ρ and the floor `F`. **Opacity is priced rather than forbidden** — a counterparty discounts what it cannot verify (OP-14), so a privacy practice becomes a property of a network's output. Three residues are flagged: **information capture by the network itself**, a **measured coverage cost** to privacy, and a network's choice **binding members who did not make it**. **§5.1d** is new: **the back-trace horizon is birth, and it runs on both sides** — a lifetime of estimated consumption arrives together with a lifetime of self-care credit, which at roughly 3,650 h/yr against roughly 1,380 h/yr makes onboarding a windfall for a median person rather than a penalty. Evidence is voluntary, moves the figure either way, may arrive at any later date, and re-derives the ledger under A6. Two conditions carry it: estimates for undisclosed periods are computed over the **undisclosed residual**, and estimates **err against the estimated party** on both sides so evidence always pays — with the self-care floor exempt, because it is credited by proof-of-life and not estimated at all. **⚠️ It escalates OP-22 sharply: a back-trace is a life dossier.** **§5.1c** is new: **the residual is held, not allocated** — a coverage gap is computed and published but charged to nobody, and a dark producer's share is back-traced to them when they onboard, which is also the only way they can transact at all. A4 is not abandoned but **pending**, and the damage is already priced through §3.3's ambient stock without anyone being charged for another's units. **§5.1b** gains the **conservative-count rule** (under-count the dark; the self-liquidating error is the safe one) and generalises the witness from production to any conserved dimension. Schema consequences are in EventLog v0.8 §4.1a/§7.4. No axiom changed. Full paper: `00-strategy/OP-26_coverage_and_closure.md`.
