## How to turn these into a share of hours

**The problem.** A ton is not an hour, in two ways. The type of food matters: a ton of beef took more work than a ton of potatoes. The stage matters more: a ton thrown away at home took farm work, factory work, transport and shop work. A ton left in the field took only the growing.

**The stage effect, with digits.** Farms are 24.2% of ReFED's surplus tons and 4% of its dollars (13.4 of 380 billion). Consumers, at home and at restaurants, are 46% of the tons and 68% of the dollars.

**The food-type effect is small.** In the ERS table the loss is 31% of the supply by weight and 29% by money. The reason is that every food group loses a similar share, 26% to 41%. Meat is 11.5% of the lost weight and 30% of the lost money, but its loss rate (26%) is the lowest of the large groups, and the two effects nearly cancel.

**What the run should do.**

1. **Use money shares, not weight shares.** Money at each stage is the nearest published stand-in for the hours spent up to that stage.
2. **Apply a loss at each stage to all the work done before it.** For food eaten at home, by ReFED's 2024 figures: homes lose 13% of what they buy, shops lose 3% of sales, makers lose 4% of sales. What is left is 0.87 × 0.97 × 0.96 = 0.81. So about **19%** of the work behind food for the home goes into food that is not eaten. For food service: 0.89 × 0.96 = 0.854, so about **15%**. This leaves out farm losses, and ReFED's dollars include some inedible parts.
3. **Run a low and a high.** High: 29% of food hours (ERS, by money, edible food only). Low: 14% (the weight-based low above), until a money-based low exists.
4. **Decide what "cut" means before the run.** The rows above are food that was not eaten. They are not food that can be saved. The one measured cut found is the UK's: edible home waste a person fell 24% in 15 years. On that basis the saving is about a quarter of the waste. 0.24 × 18% = about 4% of the food supply.

**Worked example.** The run knows the food hours in the 1,380-hour life; this note does not. Suppose food took 200 hours (a figure made up here only to show the sum). High: 0.29 × 200 = 58 hours. Low: 0.14 × 200 = 28 hours. Saving shown in practice: 0.24 × 0.18 × 200 = about 9 hours.

**Extra numbers the run needs.**

- Hours in the 1,380 that belong to food, split into farm, manufacturing, transport and shops, and restaurants. The split decides the answer.
- Total US spending on food in 2024, at home and away, to turn ReFED's 380 billion dollars into one share. Not found here.
- ReFED's dollars by cause. They would give a money-based low. They are in ReFED's Food Waste Monitor, which was not opened.

## What cannot be cut

- **Inedible parts.** Bones, shells, pits and inedible peel are 27% of UK food waste after the farm (2.5 of 8.6 million tonnes, WRAP). In UK food manufacturing they are 49%. In ReFED's US figures, trimmings and byproducts are 30.4% of surplus, about 21 million tons.
- **Cooking loss and shrinkage.** The ERS 31% includes water lost in cooking and in storage. ERS does not say how much. So the high value contains loss that nobody threw away.
- **Food thrown away for safety.** ReFED: 2.3% of surplus. ERS says some discards, such as mouldy produce and plate scraps at restaurants, are needed to keep food safe.
- **Food that was not thrown away.** 11% of US surplus was fed to animals and 2.5% was donated (ReFED). In the UK, 2.7 million tonnes went to animal feed (WRAP, a 2015 figure).
- **ERS says the saving is smaller than its own loss figure.** Its reasons: food spoils, waste is spread over 119 million households, and saving food costs work too. It gives no number.
- **No source gives a floor.** None of the sources read says what share of edible waste would remain with the best practice. ReFED says its 47 measures would deal with 20 million tons a year, which is 29% of surplus. Its largest measures by tons are composting and animal feed. Those change where waste goes and do not stop food being made.

## Where the sources disagree

- **How much US households, shops and restaurants waste.** The EPA says 66.2 million tons for 2019. ReFED's waste figures for the same three sectors add to 38.2 million tons for 2024 (23.1 + 12.4 + 2.72). The EPA figure is 73% higher. ReFED changed its household method, and older ReFED reports show more household waste. The EPA page read here does not give its definition.
- **Manufacturing.** EPA: 40 million tons. ReFED: 13.1 million tons of surplus, 6.66 of them waste. The gap is a factor of 3 to 6. A likely cause is what counts as a byproduct, and this was not checked.
- **Whether the US wastes more than Germany.** By ReFED, cut to Eurostat's scope, the US wastes about 123 kg a person, and Germany 129 to 131. US homes waste 62 kg a person and German homes 76. By the EPA, three US sectors alone waste 183 kg a person. So the answer changes with the source. This bears on the trap named in the sim design, that the comparison with Germany may already count some waste. On ReFED's figures there is no food-waste gap between the two countries to count twice.
- **The total.** ReFED: 29% of the supply, all stages, inedible parts in. ERS: 31%, shops and consumers only, inedible parts out, farms and factories out. EPA: about 35%, with studies from 73 to 152 million tonnes. The headline numbers agree, and the things they measure do not. ERS says its own figure is probably too low, because the food it counts as eaten comes to 2,547 calories a person a day.
- **Farms.** ReFED counts produce only. ERS counts no farm loss. For the UK, WRAP gives 1.6 million tonnes (range 0.9 to 3.5) and WWF gives 3.3. WRAP says the farm evidence is too weak to track progress against.
- **World.** FAO's 13.3% and UNEP's 19% cannot be added. They are shares of different totals: food produced, and food that reached consumers. UNEP includes inedible parts. The stages do not overlap, so they can be chained: 0.867 × 0.81 = 0.70, which gives about 30% lost or wasted. That is a rough figure and not a measurement. Neither number covers loss at harvest. WWF adds 1.2 billion tonnes on farms and reaches 40%.

## Limits

- **The low and the high come from different years and methods.** The high is 2010 and covers shops and consumers. The low is 2024 and covers all stages. Both are by weight.
- **The low is this note's arithmetic.** ReFED does not publish an edible or avoidable figure in the two documents read. The causes "trimmings and byproducts" and "not harvested" stand in for "cannot be cut" and "took little work". Some trimmings are edible, and some harvested crops are in other causes.
- **The US food supply of 241 million tons is derived** from ReFED's rounded 29%. A figure of 28.5% or 29.5% moves it by 4 million tons.
- **The UK edible share is used for the United States.** The diets differ. No US edible share was found.
- **The UNEP report, the WWF report and both EPA reports were not read.** Their rows are marked. The EPA range of 73 to 152 million tonnes is from a search summary.
- **The Eurostat country table was decoded by a tool.** The German sector figures should be checked against the Eurostat website.
- **The WRAP paper was read on another organisation's site.** WRAP's own pages returned errors on 2026-11-19. The paper names its authors' organisation as WRAP on every page.
- **Wants are held fixed.** If less food is wasted, people may buy other things with the hours saved. ERS makes the same point about food prices.
- **Germany's home figure includes inedible parts, and so does ReFED's.** Neither is a figure for avoidable waste.
