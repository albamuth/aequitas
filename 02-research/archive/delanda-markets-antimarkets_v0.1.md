## Why this matters to Aequitas

Supplies the historical argument for the project's core positioning: **capitalism and the market are not the same thing.** Bears on [[price-equals-cost]] (A5), [[capitalism-cannot-function]] (§7.1), and the whole public framing of what Aequitas is against. It is the answer to "so you want to abolish markets?" — no, we want to abolish the entities that were never in the market.

## Key findings

- Braudel identifies **two layers**, not one: market towns (many small producers, price from below) vs. monopoly long-distance trade and financial speculation ("capitalism," essentially anti-market).
- DeLanda: capitalism *"has always engaged in anti-competitive practices, manipulating demand and supply in a variety of ways."*
- Inside large firms, *"prices are increasingly replaced by commands as coordinating mechanisms."* Large hierarchies coordinate by managerial fiat, not price signal — so they are not market participants in any meaningful sense.
- *"There is a dialectic still very much alive between capitalism on one hand, and its antithesis, the 'non-capitalism' of the lower level."*
- On innovation: *"It was not the capitalists who brought about the first cotton revolution; all the new ideas came from enterprising small businesses."*
- *"Markets, ecosystems and decentralized networks have in common… synergistic properties [that] emerge spontaneously."*
- Cites **Galbraith**: on this definition roughly **50% of US organizations** would count as antimarket institutions, related to supply and demand only indirectly. *(Second-hand attribution — verify against Galbraith directly before citing.)*
- The distinction cuts against **both** neoliberal and Marxist theory, each of which assumes a unified capitalist system.

## What we can use

- **Framing, in the whitepaper intro:** Aequitas is pro-market and anti-capitalist, and here is 500 years of economic history saying those are compatible.
- The **price-setter vs price-taker** distinction is a clean, non-ideological way to define exactly which entities Aequitas dissolves — useful for the civic-reformer brief where "anti-capitalist" is a liability word.
- **Meshwork vs hierarchy** maps directly onto the decentralization criterion; DeLanda's spontaneous-emergence argument is usable support for the fecundity claim.

## What it gets wrong / limitations

- **Purely diagnostic — zero mechanism.** No proposal for how prices should form absent both market and command. Aequitas must supply all of that; DeLanda contributes nothing to C1–C8.
- The market/antimarket split is a **tendency claim, not a taxonomy**, and is contested among economic historians. Braudel's own three-tier model (material life / market economy / capitalism) is more nuanced than DeLanda's two-layer compression.
- DeLanda writes in a Deleuzian register that will not survive contact with an economics audience. **Cite Braudel directly for the academic paper**; use DeLanda for the popular text.
- Risk of over-claiming: real small-producer markets are also full of information asymmetry and local monopoly. "Markets are fine, capitalism is the problem" is cleaner than reality.
