# Auditor Independence and the Issuer-Pays Problem

**Type:** empirical / institutional record — used as a **counter-argument source** against an Aequitas proposal
**Subjects:** Arthur Andersen & Enron (2001–02); the credit rating agencies and the subprime crisis (2007–08); Sarbanes–Oxley (2002)
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-01
**URL:** [Arthur Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Andersen) · [Credit rating agencies and the subprime crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_rating_agencies_and_the_subprime_crisis) · [Sarbanes–Oxley Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarbanes%E2%80%93Oxley_Act) · [Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission report (full text, GPO)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf)

## Why this matters to Aequitas

**It was used to kill a claim the project was about to rely on**, which is the most valuable thing a source can do.

The trust-network model ([[distributed-auditing]]) rested on: *networks compete on accuracy, because accuracy benefits their subscribers.* The record says otherwise, repeatedly and expensively.

## The finding

> **Subscribers do not want accuracy. They want a favourable assignment. Accuracy benefits the counterparty.**

Whenever the party being assessed pays for the assessment, assessments drift favourable. This is not a claim about individual dishonesty; it is a claim about which direction the money points.

**Two well-documented instances:**

- **Audit.** Arthur Andersen was paid by Enron, and also sold it consulting services. The firm was convicted of obstruction in 2002 (later overturned on jury-instruction grounds by the US Supreme Court) and effectively ceased operating regardless. **Sarbanes–Oxley's response was structural** — restricting the non-audit services a firm may sell its audit clients — which is itself an admission that competition alone does not discipline the relationship.
- **Ratings.** Under **issuer-pays**, the agencies were compensated by the banks whose structured products they rated. AAA ratings were extended to securities that later defaulted en masse. The [FCIC](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf) treated the ratings failure as a central cause of the crisis rather than an incidental one.

## The correction it forced

**A proposed defence was rejected on this evidence.** The proposal: *trust networks are co-ops funded by member pledges, so with no profit motive they have no reason to be lenient.*

> **The conflict is directional, not monetary.** Making Arthur Andersen a co-op **owned by its clients** would have been worse than the arrangement Enron actually paid for, not better. Removing the profit motive leaves the leniency motive completely intact.

**What Aequitas does remove, and should claim:** there is no market-dominating corporation with the profit to fund captured science, because A5 removes profit in exchange. **The funding channel closes. The directional conflict does not.**

## What was adopted instead

Two structural rules, neither requiring an authority — see [[rival-sector-audit]] and Foundations §3.3a:

1. **A trust network concentrated in the sector it audits is captured by construction.** In Aequitas everyone is both producer and consumer, so a *general-membership* network is dominated by the consuming side for any given good and its incentives align automatically. **Membership composition is public in the log**, so this is a detectable screening property rather than a rule anyone must enforce.
2. **Rival-sector audit**, because rule 1 creates its own bias: a consumer-dominated network wants the debit of what its members consume to read *low*, and nobody funds the correction of an error in their own favour. **The rival sector does.**

**Read the two together. Neither is safe alone.** This is registered as **OP-24 — understatement drift.**

## What this source does *not* settle

- **Whether rival-sector audit works.** No historical analogue was found for it — it is a proposal, not a documented mechanism. **Needs a simulation.**
- **Whether reputational competition ever disciplines issuer-pays.** The subscriber-pays model existed in ratings before the 1970s; why it was displaced, and whether it was more accurate, is **not researched here and should be.**
- **The Andersen conviction's legal status.** It was overturned; the firm collapsed anyway. Do not overstate the legal finding in the academic paper — the useful fact is the *structural response* (SOX), not the verdict.
