## Why this matters to Aequitas

Aequitas's whole attribution claim is in A1: flows are *"attributable to the people who caused the movement."* That sentence is doing enormous work and currently has no philosophical grounding. **Ellerman supplies it — and simultaneously exposes its hardest limit.** He is both the best ally the project has and the source of register item P6.

## Key findings

- **The labour theory of property is the property-theoretic application of the juridical principle of responsibility**: impute legal responsibility in accordance with who was *in fact* responsible. This is not a moral claim about desert; it is the same principle courts already use for crimes, transplanted to production.
- The argument: **if labour is de facto responsible for production, labour should be de jure responsible.** Only human beings act intentionally; tools and capital do not. Responsibility therefore imputes entirely to the people, never to the machinery or its owners.
- **Inalienable rights** rest on **de facto non-transferability**: some attributes of a person cannot actually be transferred, only pretended to be. Human agency is one. This makes the employment contract invalid in the same structural way a voluntary-slavery contract is — not because it is unfair, but because the thing it purports to transfer cannot move.
- The **de facto firm** (the people actually producing) is distinct from the **de jure firm** (the legal residual claimant). No property right connects them; only a contract does, and its direction is contingent — labour could hire capital as easily as the reverse.
- He explicitly distinguishes his position from the Marxian labour theory of *value*. It is a theory of **imputation**, not of price.

## What we can use

1. **Adopt Ellerman as the grounding for attribution in place of Marx.** This is strictly better positioning: it is a juridical argument, not a value-theoretic one, so it does not inherit the transformation problem, the negative-values problem, or a century of hostile priors. It is also harder to dismiss — it appeals to a principle its opponents already accept everywhere else.
2. **It independently derives the anti-capitalist result** that Foundations §7.1 derives from price ≡ cost. Two independent routes to "no capitalist income" is a substantially stronger paper than one.
3. **It grounds A3 (non-fungibility) directly.** Credit records who was responsible; responsibility is a fact about a person and is not transferable. **A3 stops being a design choice and becomes a consequence** — a real gain in universality.
4. It supplies the correct reply to "why do the machine's owners get nothing?" — because machines do not act, and responsibility imputes only to actors.

## What it gets wrong / limitations — and where Aequitas is exposed

**The one serious problem, and it is serious.** Ellerman is explicit that de facto responsibility for a jointly produced output is **joint and non-decomposable**. The whole team is responsible for the whole product. There is no fact of the matter that says the welder caused 40% of the bridge and the surveyor 15%.

Aequitas needs exactly that number, for every collaborative event in the log.

This is **structurally identical to the joint-production problem one level up** — see `02-research/joint-production-allocation-problem.md`. Physics does not split a refinery's debit among its outputs; responsibility does not split a team's credit among its members. Register item **P6**.

The direct consequence: **hours worked is an allocation convention, not a measurement.** It may well be the right convention — it is simple, universal, gameable only in obvious ways, and consistent with A2. But the Foundations currently present it as a physical fact, and it is not one. Say so in §1.

Secondary limitations:
- Ellerman's own conclusion is **worker cooperatives**, an institutional prescription. Aequitas should take the principle and decline the institution, exactly as it declines Neurath's central planning.
- He is silent on how a co-op divides its surplus internally — precisely the question P6 asks.
