# Participatory Economics (Parecon)

**Type:** theory / contemporary model
**Author(s):** Michael Albert, Robin Hahnel
**Published:** early 1990s onward; *The Political Economy of Participatory Economics* (Princeton, 1991)
**Retrieved:** 2026-08-01
**URL:** [participatoryeconomics.info](https://participatoryeconomics.info/institutions/participatory-planning/) · [Hahnel, *In Defense of Participatory Economics*](https://znetwork.org/znetarticle/in-defense-of-participatory-economics-by-robin-hahnel/) · [Hahnel, *Participatory Economics & the Next System*](https://thenextsystem.org/sites/default/files/2017-08/NewSystems_RobinHahnel.pdf) · [*The Political Economy of Participatory Economics* (JSTOR)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv131bvr2)
**Local copy:** none — sources are open-access online.

## Why this matters to Aequitas

Parecon is the **closest living competitor**: a fully specified, non-market, non-central-planning economic model with an explicit remuneration rule and an explicit allocation procedure. It has been attacked by economists for thirty years, so its critique literature is a free stress-test of every design choice Aequitas shares with it. It also disagrees with Aequitas on the single most load-bearing question — *what labour is credited for* — which makes the comparison sharp rather than merely adjacent.

## Key findings

**Four institutions:**
1. **Worker and consumer councils** — nested, self-managing, decision weight proportional to how much you are affected.
2. **Balanced job complexes** — empowering and rote tasks distributed evenly across all workers, so no one holds a monopoly on conceptual work.
3. **Remuneration for effort and sacrifice** — pay tracks **duration, intensity, and onerousness** only. Explicitly *not* output, *not* property, *not* innate talent or bargaining power. Ratings are given by co-workers, after the work.
4. **Participatory planning** — an iterative procedure replacing markets. An Iteration Facilitation Board publishes **indicative prices** as estimates of social opportunity cost; councils submit consumption and production proposals; the board measures excess demand or supply and revises prices proportionally; proposals are resubmitted until the system converges.

**The coordinator class thesis.** Parecon's founding historical claim: Soviet-type systems abolished capitalists and produced a new ruling class regardless — *coordinators*, who monopolised planning and conceptual labour. Abolishing property income is therefore **not sufficient** to abolish class.

## What we can use

- **The coordinator-class argument is the strongest external challenge to Aequitas's positioning** (Foundations §8, "keep functional institutions, change only their economic nature") and to OP-1 (service credit → influence). Registered as **P4** in `00-strategy/Aequitas_Objections_v0.1.md`.
- **Onerousness is a dimension Aequitas has no answer for.** Parecon pays for it; Aequitas's material resolution of A2 covers exertion, skill, and hazard but not tedium or indignity. Registered as **OP-16**.
- The **indicative-price iteration** is a worked example of generating allocation signals without profit. Aequitas should be able to say why it does not need one — or adopt something like it.
- Parecon's decision rule — *weight proportional to how much you are affected* — is a fourth candidate for OP-1, alongside the three in §6.2, and it does not accumulate.

## What it gets wrong / limitations

1. **Effort is not observably measurable.** The core critique: rating a co-worker's sacrifice requires [pervasive surveillance and risks humiliation](https://znetwork.org/znetarticle/in-defense-of-participatory-economics-by-robin-hahnel/), and the numbers are unfalsifiable. **Aequitas is strictly better here** — resolving hard/skilled/hazardous labour into *material* costs (calories, training, health harm) needs no one to judge anyone's soul. This is a genuine Aequitas advantage and should be stated in the academic paper.
2. **The IFB is a Hayek target.** [Critics argue](https://ejpe.org/journal/article/view/867) that no body can plausibly announce opportunity costs for all goods, resources, labour categories, and capital stocks. **Aequitas's weighting-model maintainer is the same object wearing different clothes** — see P8.
3. **Procedural load.** Convergence requires many iterations across nested councils; critics call the structure [convoluted](https://www.communistvoice.org/32cPareconStructure.html) and the meeting burden prohibitive. Aequitas's log-based approach avoids the deliberation cost entirely — worth contrasting explicitly.
4. **Balanced job complexes read as compulsory labour discipline** to [some worker critics](http://libcom.org/blog/workers-critique-parecon-11042012). Do not adopt the mechanism uncritically just because the class analysis behind it is sound.
5. Simpler alternatives may dominate: some scholars argue equal hourly pay with capped countable hours approximates the same equity with none of the rating machinery — which is closer to Aequitas's A2 than to Parecon.
