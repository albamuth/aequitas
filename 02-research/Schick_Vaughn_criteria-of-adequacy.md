## Why this matters to Aequitas

**Foundations §2.1 says every mechanism in the system is tested against three criteria — universality, decentralization, fecundity — and until 2026-08-28 it never said where they came from or why a system derived from axioms needs criteria at all.**

**That gap was doing real damage.** A reader met a table headed *"Conformance to the Three Criteria"* with no statement of what a criterion is for. **An unattributed test looks like a house rule.** An attributed one is a standard the project can be held to by somebody who did not write it.

---

## Key findings

**Schick and Vaughn's *criteria of adequacy* judge a hypothesis on five things.** Their claim is that the amount of understanding a theory produces is set by how well it meets them, because the five measure how far a theory unifies what is already known.

| Criterion | What it asks |
|---|---|
| **Testability** | Can the hypothesis be checked against something outside itself? |
| **Fruitfulness** | Does it successfully predict phenomena nobody had looked for? |
| **Scope** | How much diverse phenomena does it account for? |
| **Simplicity** | How many assumptions does it need? |
| **Conservatism** | How well does it fit with what is already established? |

**They are taught as a set for evaluating extraordinary claims**, which is the book's subject. The set is descriptive rather than a pass mark: a hypothesis that scores badly on one may still win on the others.

## What Aequitas took, and what it did to it

**Three of the five, all renamed, and none of the renames is cosmetic.**

| Theirs | Ours | What the rename changed |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | **Universality** | **Strengthened from a quantity to an absolute.** Scope asks *how much* a theory covers. Universality asks whether **one mechanism covers every case with no exception, profession, nation, or class carved out.** A system with exceptions cannot be shown to be wrong, because any awkward case earns a new exception |
| **Testability** | **Decentralization** | **Narrowed from *can it be tested* to *who may test it*.** A claim that only a licensed body can check is testable and still useless here, because whoever holds that licence becomes the thing to capture |
| **Fruitfulness** | **Fecundity** | **Widened from prediction to upkeep.** Theirs asks whether a theory predicts new phenomena. Ours asks whether a mechanism **pays its own maintainer from inside the system** — the *"does this need a Paul Glover?"* screen, earned by Ithaca HOURS dying when its founder moved |

### Two are dropped, and one of the two is dropped on principle

- **Simplicity is not claimed.** The accounting is not simple, and pretending otherwise would be a claim the documents cannot support.
- **Conservatism is the one criterion this project cannot use.** It asks how well a hypothesis fits established belief. **The whole proposal is that established belief about cost is wrong**, so scoring well on conservatism would be evidence against the theory rather than for it.

> **That second point is worth stating rather than passing over.** Dropping a criterion because it is inconvenient is exactly what an unfalsifiable theory does. **The defence is that the other three get harder, not easier**, and that the project says in public which one it dropped and why.

## What is still unresolved

**Nobody has confirmed that this is the book the criteria actually came from.** Three other candidates are close enough to be worth naming, and none of them matches all three words:

| Candidate | Its set | Why it does not settle it |
|---|---|---|
| [Thomas Kuhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kuhn), *Objectivity, Value Judgment, and Theory Choice* (1977) | accuracy, consistency, **scope**, simplicity, **fruitfulness** | **Two of the three, and no testability criterion at all** |
| [Quine and Ullian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Web_of_Belief), *The Web of Belief* (1970) | conservatism, modesty, simplicity, **generality**, **refutability** | Generality and refutability map, but **nothing maps to fecundity** |
| [Imre Lakatos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Lakatos), research programmes (1970) | a programme is *progressive* if it predicts novel facts | **Fecundity alone.** Not a set of three |

**Schick and Vaughn is the only one of the four that carries all three ideas in one list.** That is the whole of the argument for it, and it is circumstantial.

**How to close it.** Ask the author what they were reading when the OFCS criteria were written. **A memory is better evidence here than a match.**
