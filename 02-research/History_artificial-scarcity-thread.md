## Why this matters to Aequitas

Foundations section 4.6 says that much scarcity is produced, and it cites no source for that. This thread supplies sources, cases with digits, and a hostile witness. It also supplies the strongest replies. The writer who coined the phrase held that natural scarcity is permanent, and he blamed trade unions first. The project's own measurement in section 3.5 says working hours fall short. A note that left those out would be cheerleading.

## Key findings

**Who wrote what, and when**

- **The phrase is W. H. Hutt's.** He wrote of "the deliberate contrivance of scarcities" in *Economists and the Public: A Study of Competition and Opinion* (London: Jonathan Cape, 1936). Boudreaux quotes it from page 344 of the 1990 Transaction Publishers reprint. Hutt's sentence says that ending these made scarcities could end what people then called physical poverty.
- **The distinction is one year older.** Ebeling's note 4 cites Hutt, "Natural and Contrived Scarcities", *South African Journal of Economics*, September 1935, pages 345 to 353 (no link found; not opened). I do not know if the exact phrase is in that article.
- **Hutt's two kinds of scarcity.** *Natural scarcity* is the plain fact that means are fewer than wants. *Contrived scarcity* is a shortage made when owners of a resource hold part of it back and block rivals from entering. Both definitions are from Ebeling's and Egger's summaries, not from Hutt's page.
- **Hutt was a free-market economist.** He was English, taught at the University of Cape Town from 1928, and joined the Mont Pelerin Society. He coined *consumers' sovereignty* in the same 1936 book. Source: [Wikipedia, "W. H. Hutt"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._H._Hutt).
- **Boudreaux's piece is about 113 words.** It holds one quotation and two sentences of comment. It names two living mechanisms: tariffs and occupational licensing (a legal rule that only people with a state permit may do a job). It says many made scarcities have been ended or reduced in the 80 years since 1936. It gives no count and no case.
- **Wikipedia's definition.** Artificial scarcity is scarcity of a good although the means to make or share enough of it exist. Wikipedia names monopoly pricing as the main cause, helped by laws that block rivals or by high fixed costs.

**Whom each source blames**

| Source | Politics | Whom it blames | What it defends |
|---|---|---|---|

| Boudreaux (2016, 2018) | Free-market | The state, and both conservatives and progressives who back tariffs and licensing | Free trade, open entry to jobs |
| Kinsella (2008) | Right-libertarian | Statute law. Patents and copyright are grants of monopoly by the state | Private property in every physical good |
| Tucker (1888) | Individualist anarchist | Four monopolies upheld by the state: money, land, tariff, patent. Capital lobbied for them | Free competition with no privilege |
| Kropotkin (1892) | Anarchist communist | Owners of land, mines and machines, and the state that enforces their claims | Common ownership |
| Cox (1998) | Socialist (Socialist Party of Great Britain) | Production for profit as such | Production for use |
| Wikipedia | Mixed | Monopoly pricing, by firms and by law | Nothing; it lists both sides |

**Where the free-market version and the anarchist version agree**

- Scarcity made by restriction is a different thing from scarcity given by nature.
- Patents and copyright make ideas scarce by law. Copying an idea takes it from nobody.
- Tariffs, quotas, cartels and entry barriers hold supply down and prices up.
- Tucker links the two camps. He blames state privilege, as Hutt does. He expects interest, rent and profit to fall close to nothing once the privilege ends, which is the result Aequitas wants.

**Where they disagree**

- **Profit.** Hutt treats profit under open entry as the signal that draws new makers in and ends a shortage. Cox and Kropotkin treat profit as the cause of the shortage. Tucker treats profit as an effect of privilege.
- **Trade unions.** They are Hutt's main culprit. He held that a strike threat lifts wages above what clears the market and leaves other workers idle. The anarchist writers treat unions as defence.
- **Property in physical things.** Kinsella defends it because physical things are scarce. Kropotkin attacks it.
- **Natural scarcity.** Hutt holds it is permanent. Cox holds that unlimited wants are a habit of this society and not a fact of human nature.

**Cases with digits**

- **Brazil, 1931 to 1932.** Brazil's National Coffee Council burned 6,565,641 sacks of coffee by June 1932. Each sack held 132 lb, so that is about 867 million lb (my arithmetic). The plan was 18 million sacks by 1 January 1933, worth about 117,000,000 dollars at 6.50 dollars a sack. The New York price of Brazilian coffee rose about 40% while other commodity prices stayed low. Source: *Time*, opened.
- **The Phoebus lamp cartel.** Founded in Geneva on 23 December 1924 by Osram, Philips, Compagnie des Lampes, Tungsram, Associated Electrical Industries and Tokyo Electric, with General Electric tied in through subsidiaries. Household lamps had lasted 1,500 to 2,000 hours. The cartel set a standard of 1,000 hours. A central laboratory in Switzerland tested samples, and a factory paid a fine when its lamps lasted too long. The average life fell from 1,800 hours in 1926 to 1,205 hours in 1933–34. Sales rose from 335.7 million lamps in 1926–27 to 420.8 million four years later. Source: Krajewski, who read the Osram archives in Berlin; opened.
- **Kropotkin's cases, 1892.** Oysters thrown into the sea to keep them dear. Coal miners held to three days a week to keep coal dear. He states that England's population grew 62% from 1844 to 1890 while its output grew about 130%.
- **Generic drugs.** Wikipedia cites the United States [Food and Drug Administration, "Generic Drug Facts"](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/generic-drugs/generic-drug-facts) for the finding that prices fall when several generic makers enter. Not opened. Check it before citing.

## What we can use

- **A hostile witness for section 4.6.** The first row of the table in section 4.6 says much scarcity is produced. Hutt said so in 1936 as a defender of markets, and Boudreaux repeated it on a free-market site in 2016. Citing them answers the reply that only socialists believe it.
- **Hutt's split matches the cost rule.** A cost figure under A5 reports only what a thing used up. That is Hutt's natural scarcity. A held-back supply cannot change the figure. Worked case: Brazil burned 6,565,641 sacks and the price rose about 40%. Under A5 the figure for a sack of coffee stays at the work and material that sack took, burned stock or not.
- **Two cases with digits for section 4.6.** Brazil shows a state destroying supply. Phoebus shows firms cutting quality by agreement, from 1,800 hours to 1,205 hours, with fines for lamps that lasted.
- **Tucker for sections 4.5 and 5.1.** In 1888 an anarchist named patents, tariffs, land titles and the money monopoly as the four sources of interest, rent and profit. This gives the no-patent rule a 138-year pedigree beside [Proudhon](Proudhon_mutualism.md) and [Warren](Warren_cost-the-limit-of-price.md).
- **A second argument for section 4.5.** Section 4.5 argues that with no profit, an exclusive right has nothing to protect. Kinsella argues from a different premise: a rule of exclusion is only needed where one person's use takes the thing from another, and copying an idea takes nothing. The two arguments do not depend on each other.
- **A test of the author's claim that can fail.** Section 3.5 puts world need at about 10.4 trillion labour-hours a year for a typical United States life, against about 6.5 trillion available. The gap is 3.9 trillion, which is 37.5% of the need. A typical United States life takes 1,380 hours a year, and 37.5% of that is about 518 hours. **So the claim that capacity already exceeds consumption passes at that standard only if at least 518 of the 1,380 hours are waste that can be cut with no loss.** At the method of the more efficient rich countries, where the need is 8.7 to 9.1 trillion hours, the bar is 25% to 29%. Neither source measures this share. The test also leaves out energy, which section 3.5 puts at 0.19 of need.
- **One exploit to stress-test.** The Phoebus firms gained from volume: more lamps sold. A5 removes the margin on each lamp. It is worth checking that no maker under Aequitas gains anything else from selling more short-lived goods.

## What it gets wrong / limitations

- **The Boudreaux piece is a quotation, not a survey.** It has no evidence, no numbers and two examples. Everything it claims about poverty rests on one sentence written in 1936.
- **I did not read Hutt.** Every statement here about his views comes from Boudreaux (an ally), Ebeling (an admirer, writing for the same foundation) and Egger (friendly but critical). Hutt's page 344 may name culprits that these three leave out.
- **No source measures how much scarcity is made.** None gives a share of output, a share of hours, or a count of people. Hutt's sentence is a judgment. Cox says well over half the workforce in rich countries does useless work, and gives no source and no year.
- **The Wikipedia article is thin.** It has 12 references. Its entries on hoarding, limited editions and migration cite nothing. Its whole left-wing section rests on one 1998 article in a party magazine.
- **The crop cases are old and were run by states.** Brazil in 1932 and the United States in 1933 acted in a depression, on behalf of growers. Wikipedia gives no numbers for the later European and United States surpluses.
- **The free-market sources would count Aequitas as a restriction.** Hutt's cure for made scarcity is open entry and a price that moves. Aequitas fixes the figure at cost. By Hutt's reasoning, as Ebeling reports it, a fixed figure cannot ration a good that is short by nature. Section 4.6 already says this question is open.
- **Egger makes the anarchist point against Hutt.** He notes that Hutt's picture of an unhampered market ignores how property rules decide what is available and on what terms. That is the gap Tucker and Kropotkin write about.
- **Hutt's politics.** He attacked the apartheid job colour bar in 1964 as a device of white trade unions. Wikipedia also records that he opposed equal universal suffrage and favoured a weighted vote. Cite the economics and do not hold him up as a model.
- **Kropotkin's numbers are 1892 rhetoric.** They are not set against any measured standard of living.

## Against the claim that scarcity is artificial

The claim under test: scarcity today is largely artificial, capacity exceeds consumption, much is hoarded by a few, and much is wasted.

- **The project's own measurement contradicts the strong form.** Section 3.5 finds the world's hours reach 0.63 of what a typical United States life for everyone needs, and 0.72 to 0.75 at the method of the more efficient rich countries. United States deployable hours reach 0.43 to 0.87 of need. Energy reaches 0.19. These are natural limits at that standard. The claim can only hold at a lower standard, or if a large share of today's consumption is waste, and neither source shows either.
- **The man who coined the phrase held that natural scarcity is permanent.** Ebeling reports that Hutt called it an inescapable part of the human condition. Hutt's claim was that made scarcity deepens poverty. He did not claim that scarcity itself is made.
- **The mainstream definition does not depend on any restriction.** Robbins (1932) defined economics as the study of ends set against scarce means that have other uses. Samuelson called scarcity a basic fact of life. Both are cited by [Wikipedia, "Scarcity"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarcity); neither was opened. An hour spent on one thing is not spent on another, with or without patents. Aequitas agrees: section 0 treats a person's 24 hours a day as the finite thing.
- **Boudreaux says many made scarcities are already gone.** If so, their removal over 80 years did not end scarcity. Either the ones left are very large or Hutt's 1936 sentence overstated the case. The piece does not say which.
- **Made scarcity breaks down without legal backing.** The Phoebus agreement was written to last until 1955. Sales fell more than 20% between 1930 and 1933, General Electric's basic patents expired in 1929, 1930 and 1933, and the agreement was annulled in 1940. Bulow (1986), as Wikipedia reports him, found that product life grows as competition grows. Wikipedia adds that longer-lived Japanese cars forced United States makers to build better. This supports the free-market account, in which rivals end the scarcity, more than the account that blames profit.
- **Some of the shortening is a real trade-off.** The cartel said a hotter filament gives more light per watt and a shorter life. Krajewski rejects that as the motive but does not deny the physics. Kotler, as Wikipedia reports him, held that much so-called planned obsolescence is ordinary rivalry and technical change.
- **Some made scarcity protects a real limit.** Wikipedia's case for artificial scarcity says a cap on output can stop a source from being used up. A fishing quota is a made scarcity that guards a natural one.
- **Creation has a real cost even when copying is free.** Wikipedia's case for patents is that a drug is dear to develop and cheap to make. Ending the patent does not end the development hours. Aequitas still has to find those hours through pledges.
- **Some goods are short by nature.** One original painting, one seafront. No accounting rule makes more of them.
- **The sources document restriction, not hoarding by a few.** Hutt's main culprit is the trade union, which is many workers. The crop destroyers were governments. Wikipedia lists hoarding with no case. On war, luxury and dumping the sources give no figures at all.
- **What the sources do support.** Laws that make concentration worse are documented in kind by every source: patents, copyright, tariffs, licensing. None of them gives the size of the effect.
