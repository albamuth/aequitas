## Why this matters to Aequitas

Veblen supplies two things at once: a name for the kind of consumption Aequitas's permanent-consumption-debit is designed to expose, and the intellectual seed of the Technocracy movement (see [Technocracy: energy accounting](../00-strategy/GLOSSARY.md#src-technocracy-energy-accounting)) — the nearest historical relative Aequitas has to define itself against. He's a bridge node: he connects the critique of *display-wealth* to the idea of running production for use rather than profit.

Bears on: consumption/pollution debit ([Foundations §3.2](../00-strategy/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.13.md#32-the-two-kinds-of-debit--and-the-two-components-of-property-debit)) and the claim that capitalism can't operate inside the system ([Foundations §7.1](../00-strategy/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.13.md#71-capitalism-cannot-function)).

## Key findings

- **Conspicuous consumption.** In *The Theory of the Leisure Class* Veblen argued that people display status through *wasteful* spending and leisure — consuming to be *seen* consuming, not to meet a need. Wealth signals itself by visible waste.
- **Institutional economics.** He founded the view that the economy is shaped by *social institutions and habits*, not by rational agents optimising in a vacuum — culture drives economic behaviour.
- **Industry vs. business — his most durable idea.** Veblen split *industry* (engineers and workers, who make useful things efficiently) from *business* (owners and financiers, whose interest is pecuniary — restricting output and manipulating markets to make money). The two pull in opposite directions. This dichotomy directly seeded the **Technocracy movement**, which imagined engineers displacing the business class to run production for use.

## What we can use

- **"Conspicuous consumption" names what Aequitas's ledger makes visible.** In Aequitas, consumption and pollution debit is *permanent* and sits on whoever caused it. Wasteful display consumption isn't hidden behind a price tag — it shows up, forever, as a heavy record. Where Veblen could only *describe* the sociology of waste, Aequitas *accounts* for it. See [Foundations §3.2](../00-strategy/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.13.md#32-the-two-kinds-of-debit--and-the-two-components-of-property-debit).
- **The industry/business split prefigures Aequitas's core positioning.** Veblen's "business" — the pecuniary layer that restricts output to make money — is close to Braudel's *antimarket* (see [DeLanda: markets & antimarkets](../00-strategy/GLOSSARY.md#src-delanda-markets-antimarkets)) and to what Aequitas removes: the profit-in-exchange layer, leaving the making-things layer intact. See [Foundations §7.1](../00-strategy/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.13.md#71-capitalism-cannot-function).
- **He is the honest lineage of Technocracy** — which lets Aequitas locate itself precisely: it shares Veblen's respect for physical production over pecuniary manipulation, but *rejects* the Technocratic conclusion (rule by engineers), which is exactly the capture Aequitas's "no organisation owns the core" rule guards against. See [Technocracy: energy accounting](../00-strategy/GLOSSARY.md#src-technocracy-energy-accounting) and [Foundations §A8](../00-strategy/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.13.md#a8-no-governing-body).

## What it gets wrong / limitations — from Aequitas's angle

- **The engineers-should-rule idea is the trap, not the model.** Veblen's industry/business split slides easily into "let the technical experts take over" — the Technocratic error. Aequitas keeps the respect for real production but refuses the elite; nobody rules the core.
- **Diagnosis, not mechanism.** Veblen is a brilliant critic with no accounting system. Aequitas supplies what he described.
- **Conspicuous consumption is sociology, not measurement.** Veblen observed the *motive*; Aequitas doesn't try to read motives — it just records the physical consumption, whatever the reason. That's a feature: no one has to judge *why* you consumed, only *that* you did.
