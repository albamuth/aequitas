## Verdict
**The lever is real and small: about 0.12 h a year for a typical two-adult household (0.0045% of its 2,760 h debit), and at most 22.6 h a year (0.82%) in the worst corner swept.** These are hand figures. Python was refused again, so the code and its 8 self-tests are on disk and have not run. The size is small for one reason that follows from Foundations §3.4a. Under the max rule, routing saves `min(f_A, f_B)` times the hours of the steps both co-products passed. That is never more than the smaller holding's carry on those steps, and it is zero unless the two holdings point at the same parcel. Two more facts keep it small. Shop beef and imported leather almost never share a steer (about 2 × 10⁻⁷ h/yr). And if both adults hold some of both co-products, each account already takes the max inside itself, so routing saves nothing. On the goods it touches the lever is large: 13% for petrol and propane, 41% for a farm-share steer's beef and its hide. That matches what Wubbitys said ("halves"). On the household it is under 1% at every corner. Under the sum rule the saving is 0, as declared. Nothing in the documents moves.

## What this does not show
1. **That the code is right.** It has never run.
2. **The same-parcel share `q` is declared, not measured.** This is the share of a holding that points at the same parcels as the other co-product. It is 0.2 for propane and 0.5 for heating oil. The ceiling `q` = 1 is reported in the worst corner.
3. **The hours per steer (15–40 h) and per household's petrol (2–16 h) are scaled from ERM-level figures.** ERM is the Bureau of Labor Statistics employment requirements matrix. These hours were not re-measured per process.
4. **Labour only.** Fuel's combustion CO₂ rides each litre (Foundations §3.2b, line 3: "the pollution a thing will cause when used moves with the thing"). So it is never a joint cost and is not in the saving.
5. **The 2,760 h denominator is today's median US consumption.** It is one reading.
