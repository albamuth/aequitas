# Simulation night — 2026-10-24

## Verdict
**A false passage is worth a lot and is invisible to all three checks as published.** On the 3.4a steer a one-step-early lie cuts the worst product's figure by 0.75 MJ/kg (38% of 2.00 MJ/kg); the worst lie of any depth on a shared step cuts hooves-and-head by 3.00 of 4.67 MJ/kg (64%). On the DOE refinery, dropping one really-used shared step cuts a product's figure by 5% to 36%. The totals reconciliation catches 0 of 19 shared-step lies (6 steer, 13 refinery), and the coarse-reading ceiling catches 0 of 25 (12 steer, 13 refinery), because the ceiling bounds a figure from above and a lie lowers it. In 6 of 6 steer cases the true and false worlds publish identical totals, union and ceiling records. Only a mass meter, independent of the producer, on a boundary the lie crosses separates them: 99.7–100% caught at 0.5–2% meter noise, but 0.3–1.7% (the false-alarm rate) if the producer owns the meter. Lies that orphan a step (all on the beef) are caught by the totals check 6 of 6, and cost the producer, not the buyer. This moves 3.4a's "Not measured" to "measured: invisible without an independent boundary meter", and it shows that conformance row 7 only helps if it is run at a finer cut than the plant's own.

## What this does not show
1. The meter model catches a lie only through mass at a boundary. A lie that leaves no mass at a reachable boundary is not modelled.
2. "Independent" is assumed. This shows what a third-party meter would catch, not that one can exist, which is coywolf's actual question.
3. The digits are the illustrative 3.4a steer and a declared refinery passage table. The energy-to-hours weight is a placeholder.
4. One lie, one producer. Collusion with a buyer, or a liar who also falsifies the mass flow sheet, is not simulated.
5. The "gain" is the drop in a published figure. Aequitas has no price, so who benefits and by how much is not measured.
6. Foundations §4.5 to the end was not read tonight (about 40% of it).
