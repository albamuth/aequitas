# Simulation night — 2026-11-08

## Verdict

**Adding births and deaths lowers ρ\* at every floor, so cell 1 holds and cell 2 does not.** Without births ρ\* is 1.017 / 0.330 / 0.253 at F = 2 / 10 / 14. With births it is **0.866 / 0.262 / 0.192**, a change of −0.152 / −0.068 / −0.061 (−15% / −21% / −24%). Cell 3 holds: ρ\* at F = 2 is 0.866, below 0.90. **But most of the F = 10 and 14 drop is not births.** A control with no births and children under ruling 7 gives 0.266 and 0.194. The cause is that a child carries no debit, so at 18 they hold 18 years of floor credit and no debit. Only at F = 2 do births matter on their own (−0.128 beyond ruling 7). Two limits: this run drops a child's consumption from the books instead of moving it to the guardians, which lowers ρ\* by an amount not measured; and capacity is a declared convention that the result depends on.

## What this does not show

1. **A child's consumption is dropped, not moved to the guardians.** Ruling 7 puts about 552 h a year per child on the guardians. This run puts it on nobody. That lowers ρ\*. How much is not measured.
2. **Capacity is a convention.** It scales with the number of requesters. A fixed capacity, or one that counts everybody alive, makes rationing impossible to fail once children stop requesting (adults want at most 52,467 against 56,446). Those two rows were excluded as entailed.
3. **The population shrinks 15% and both demographic inputs are illustrative.** Neither is a sourced life table or fertility rate. F = 2 moves from 0.893 to 0.845 across birth rates 0.008 to 0.016.
4. **The F = 2 row carries the open defect in the README.** Accounts with negative room are refused in full, essentials included. F = 2 may be understated.
5. Everyone joins on day one with a flat rebuilt past. No households, no migration, one population seed.
