# Simulation night — 2026-10-17

## Verdict
**Labelling is stable once most producers do it, and it does not start on its own.** In the headline cell (20 producers, 15% discount on unlabelled figures, a label exposing 40% of the figure) one producer who drops the label loses, and the break-even discount is **0.0256**, against the brief's prior of 0.024. But a **lone** labeller ends with 3.60% of trade where its unlabelled rivals hold 5.02%, because every rival can aim at the one visible target. Labelling only grows once about **4 of 20** producers (share 0.20) already label; the discount needed to start from one labeller is **0.62**, 24 times the stay threshold, and it grows with the number of rivals (2.41 at 50 producers, none up to 3.0 at 200). The brief's refutation threshold (labelling collapses in more than half the cells) is **not crossed** when the market starts at half labelling: 17.7% of cells. It is crossed in spirit when the market starts at zero: the lone labeller fails to start in 77% of cells at 20 producers. The brief's own worked example overstates the labelling edge: returning the attackers' gains to the attackers cuts it from 4.2 points to 1.5. The flattering answer ("labelling always wins") is not what came back.

Rivals who aim **blind** (no use of the label) remove the start-up barrier: invade threshold ≈ 0. The barrier exists only because the label lets rivals single out one producer. Over the brief's grid (attack-active cells, `d` > 0): lone labeller can invade in 22.9% of cells at N = 20, 7.7% at N = 50, 40.8% at N = 10. ## What this does not show
1. **Not that labelling is stable in a real market.** The discount is a fixed input; in life it moves with how many label. Total demand is also fixed.
2. **Not that a challenge finds anything.** The success chance is an input, not tied to whether the modelled part is wrong. A producer with a right model would pay nothing for the label.
3. **Not what Aequitas's own rule does.** §4.4's label is required, `not identified` is the default; here producers may drop it. Only the stay threshold carries over.
4. **The attack prize is private** (the attacker keeps the trade). §3.3a says audit benefits are shared among rivals. Shared benefit would shrink the start-up barrier. Not run.
5. **Rivals are myopic and independent**, one good, one repeated round, no reputation. Full list of eight in RESULTS.md.
