# Q6 — Who delivers a first-world standard with the least labour? (2022)

**Scripts:** [`track6_country_labour.py`](track6_country_labour.py) (EXIOBASE multi-country embodied-labour solve) + [`track6_efficiency.py`](track6_efficiency.py) (efficiency ranking, 3 self-tests green). Answers the author's hypothesis: *the US-median standard looks unaffordable only because the American way of producing it is wildly inefficient — other rich countries deliver a comparable material life for far less labour.*

## The finding: the hypothesis holds, hard

**Embodied labour-hours per capita/yr in household consumption** (EXIOBASE 3, domestic + imported), for genuinely affluent countries:

| Country | h/cap | Life exp 2022 | Material std (AIC, EU=100) | h per life-year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **US** | **1,283** | **77.4** | 150 | **16.6** |
| UK | 1,007 | 80.4 | 113 | 12.5 |
| Norway | 872 | 83.2 | 131 | 10.5 |
| Japan | 862 | 84.0 | 108 | 10.3 |
| France | 856 | 82.3 | 113 | 10.4 |
| Sweden | 832 | 83.0 | 111 | 10.0 |
| Germany | 820 | 80.6 | 119 | 10.2 |
| Australia | 807 | 84.5 | 116 | 9.6 |
| Italy | 767 | 83.0 | 99 | 9.2 |
| **Spain** | **709** | **83.2** | 91 | **8.5** |

> **The US commands 50–80% more human labour per person than Germany, Sweden, France, or Japan — while living 3–7 years less and consuming only modestly more.** The extra American labour buys neither longer life nor proportional material welfare. It is, to first order, waste and inefficiency — exactly the hypothesis.

**Most efficient at delivering an affluent life per labour-hour:** Spain, Italy, Japan (top-tier life expectancy at ~55–67% of US hours). **Best material-standard-per-hour:** Germany, Sweden, France, Canada. **Least efficient rich country, on every metric: the US.**

*(Poland/Czechia score lowest on raw h/life-year, but that's partly because they consume less — lower AIC — not purely greater efficiency. Controlling for affluence, Southern Europe + Japan + Germany/Sweden are the true efficiency leaders.)*

## Physical cross-check: embodied CO₂ (the fossil-inefficiency leg)

EXIOBASE's air-emissions satellite *is* populated (unlike energy). Embodied **fossil CO₂** (combustion + cement + lime + fossil-waste; biogenic excluded) in household consumption, per capita/yr:

| Country | h/cap | **tCO₂/cap** | kgCO₂ per labour-hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US** | 1,283 | **11.2** | **8.8** |
| Canada | 676 | 5.3 | 7.8 |
| Germany | 820 | 4.6 | 5.6 |
| Italy | 767 | 4.5 | 5.9 |
| Japan | 862 | 4.4 | 5.1 |
| UK | 1,007 | 4.3 | 4.2 |
| **Sweden** | 832 | **3.2** | 3.8 |
| **France** | 856 | **3.2** | 3.8 |
| **Spain** | 709 | **2.7** | 3.8 |

> **The US embodies ~2.5–4× the CO₂ per person of the efficient countries — and ~2× more CO₂ per labour-hour.** So it's not only that Americans consume more; each *hour* of labour behind their consumption is roughly twice as carbon-dirty (more fossil energy per unit). France/Sweden are cleanest partly via low-carbon electricity (nuclear/hydro); Spain via renewables + leaner consumption — all legitimate faces of "the US method is unsustainable."

**This is the physical wall Q1 flagged, made concrete.** A universal *US-method* standard blows through any carbon budget ~4× over; a universal *Spanish/French-method* standard of comparable material comfort emits ~a quarter as much — the difference between fitting inside a climate-safe budget and not. The Q1 energy/materials ceiling recedes dramatically under efficient provision.

## This FLIPS the "can the world afford it?" answer

Earlier we found a *US-efficiency* median standard for all 8.1B needs ~10.4 trillion labour-h/yr — ~60% more than the world's ~6.5 T — i.e. impossible without a ~50–58 h workweek. Re-run with an **efficient** production model:

| Standard for all 8.1B, produced the way... | h/cap | World labour needed | vs current ~6.5 T |
|---|---|---|---|
| the **US** does it | 1,283 | 10.4 T | **+60% (deficit)** |
| **Germany / Sweden / Japan** do it | ~830 | 6.7 T | **≈ break-even** |
| **Spain / Italy** do it | ~740 | 6.0 T | **−8% (surplus)** |

**And that is BEFORE stripping Q2 waste** (17–36% of world labour is capture/guard/war producing nothing). Remove it too, and an affluent European-standard life for every human on Earth is comfortably deliverable **below today's ~37 h/week average workweek.**

> **The standard was never the problem. The American *method* of producing it is.** A universal decent-affluent standard is out of reach only if the whole world adopts US-style sprawl, car-dependence, long adversarial supply chains, and a financialised/admin-heavy economy. Produced the German, Spanish, or Japanese way, it fits inside the labour the world already performs.

## Why Aequitas drives this shift automatically

Every source of US inefficiency is a *cost* Aequitas prices and the dollar hides:

- **Fossil reliance** → pollution debit (A4 / §4) the efficient low-carbon version doesn't carry.
- **Long supply chains** → more transport + logistics labour-debit than local production.
- **Adversarial overhead** (healthcare admin, finance, insurance, marketing, guard labour — the bloated Track-1 categories: healthcare 145h, trade 132h) → labour with no material output, that a lean system simply doesn't spend.

Under A5 (cost, not price), the efficient method is **cheaper in the ledger by construction.** No mandate, no carbon tax — the accounting itself rewards exactly the efficiency the leaders already demonstrate.

## Honest caveats

- **Mean, not median.** EXIOBASE gives total household consumption ÷ population (a mean). Median needs per-country consumption inequality; the US is *more* unequal, so on a median basis the US looks **even worse** (its median sits further below its mean than the Nordics').
- **AIC is monetary** (PPP index) — shown for material-standard reference, distorted by price levels; the life-expectancy axis is the money-free one and tells the same story.
- **Trade-hub distortion:** Ireland (1,781 h, 99% foreign), Netherlands, Belgium, Switzerland, Denmark are inflated by transit / transfer-pricing and excluded from the affluent ranking (shown flagged in the script output).
- **Energy satellite empty; CO₂ used instead.** EXIOBASE 2022's *energy* satellite is all zeros in this vintage, so the physical cross-check uses the *populated* air-emissions (fossil CO₂) satellite above. External energy figures corroborate (US ~290 GJ/cap vs Germany/France ~150, UK ~110 — [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/energy)). The CO₂ result (US 11.2 t vs Spain 2.7) tracks the energy story tightly.
- **Productivity frozen at 2022.** No efficiency-improvement trend modelled.
