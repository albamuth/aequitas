==============================================================================
CROSS-NETWORK SPLITTING -- is the residual estimate tight against it?
==============================================================================

  Terms, before any number:
    k          how many networks one person holds an account with
    rho        the debit tolerance in the gate D <= rho*C.  1.20 here
    F          the floor, hours a day credited for staying alive.  10
    escape     what a splitter carried, as a multiple of the cap
               ONE network's books would have allowed them
    coverage   Y / N -- the share of the extent's real consumption
               this network's records captured

  Population        20,000 people, seed 11
  Median lifestyle  1,380 debit-h/yr (measured)
  Floor credit      3,650 h/yr
  Subscribes to no network   20% of the extent

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1 -- WHAT THE SPLIT IS WORTH BEFORE ANY ESTIMATE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Credit is recorded on EVERY network the person holds an account
  with -- each sees the same 24-hour day and credits its own floor.
  Debit lands on ONE network per transaction, chosen by the seller.
  So the gate is checked against a divided debit and a whole credit.

   k networks   share each sees   escape factor
        1              1.00           1.00x
        2              0.50           2.00x
        3              0.33           3.00x
        4              0.25           4.00x
        5              0.20           5.00x
       10              0.10          10.00x

  An uneven split is worse for the attacker, because the network
  with the LARGEST share is the one whose gate binds first:

   split          escape factor
   0.50 / 0.50        2.00x
   0.60 / 0.40        1.67x
   0.70 / 0.30        1.43x
   0.80 / 0.20        1.25x
   0.90 / 0.10        1.11x
   1.00 / 0.00        1.00x

  So k = 2 and an even split is the whole attack, and it is worth
  exactly 2.00x. Nothing subtler helps.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2 -- HOW LARGE AN ESTIMATE HAS TO BE TO CLOSE THE GAP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  A network closes the gap when its estimate of a subscriber's
  undisclosed activity is (k-1)/k of that subscriber's own cap.

   k    estimate needed, as a share of rho*C    in hours at rho*C = 5,580 h
   2              0.50                        2,790 h
   3              0.67                        3,720 h
   4              0.75                        4,185 h
   5              0.80                        4,464 h

  Read the k = 2 row as the finding it is: to stop a two-way split,
  a network must charge every splitter an estimated HALF of their
  entire allowance, on no evidence that they split at all.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 4 -- THE SPLIT RATIO, WITH THE ATTACKER PLAYING BEST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  At each ratio the attacker consumes the most the WORST-placed
  network will still clear:  d_max = (rho*C - est) / s,  where s is
  the largest share any one network sees. The escape factor is that
  against rho*C. A ratio of 1.00 is not a split and must read 1.00x.

   split ratio      R0        R1        R2        R3
   0.50 / 0.50      2.00x    1.78x    2.00x    1.00x
   0.60 / 0.40      1.67x    1.51x    1.67x    0.83x
   0.70 / 0.30      1.43x    1.31x    1.43x    0.71x
   0.80 / 0.20      1.25x    1.15x    1.25x    0.62x
   0.90 / 0.10      1.11x    1.03x    1.11x    0.56x
   1.00 / 0.00      1.00x    0.93x    1.00x    0.50x

  Every rule tracks 1/s almost exactly, which is the no-estimate
  line. None of them bends it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 5 -- THE ONE BOUND THAT IS REAL, AND IT IS NOT AN ESTIMATE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Foundations 4.0, fact 2: a transaction lands on the network THE
  SELLER accepts. So a buyer cannot route a purchase to network B
  unless a seller of that thing is on network B.

  The attacker's reachable split is therefore capped by the share of
  their sellers who are on the second network.

   sellers also on network B    best split reachable    escape factor
                      2%              0.02           1.02x
                      5%              0.05           1.05x
                     10%              0.10           1.11x
                     25%              0.25           1.33x
                     50%              0.50           2.00x

  Worked, k = 2. A person whose sellers are 5% on network B can put
  5% of their purchases there. Their worst network still sees 95%,
  so they gain 1.05x, not 2.00x. **The 2.00x figure assumes half of
  every seller they use accepts the second network.**

  This bound is structural and needs no estimate, no detection and
  no new rule. It is also nobody's design -- it holds only while
  seller bases stay unevenly split, and it weakens as networks even
  out. It is a fact about an early ecosystem, not a defence.

==============================================================================
WHAT THIS FOUND
==============================================================================

  1. THE GAP IS REAL AND IT IS EXACTLY 1/s, WORTH k AT AN EVEN SPLIT.
     Credit duplicates across networks; debit divides. Two accounts
     evenly split buy 2.00x the intended allowance. An uneven split
     is strictly worse for the attacker, so there is nothing subtler
     to look for.

  2. THE RESIDUAL ESTIMATE AS WRITTEN CLOSES NONE OF IT.
     Foundations 4.4 divides the residual over UNMEASURED PRODUCERS.
     A splitter is measured -- the network holds a real record of
     their half -- and 'a record always beats an estimate' then stops
     the network overriding its own record. The rule that protects an
     honest subscriber from a bad guess is the rule a splitter hides
     behind.

  3. THE SPLITTER'S SIGNATURE IS NOT LOW CONSUMPTION, AND EVERY
     COHORT RULE IS AIMED AT THE WRONG SHAPE.
     This is the finding the first run got backwards. A splitter
     records rho*C on EACH network -- their books show a HEAVY
     consumer sitting exactly at their limit, well ABOVE the cohort
     median. R2 tops up whoever records BELOW their cohort, so it
     fires on nobody who splits and on half of everybody who is
     simply frugal. Measured: 0% of splitters caught, 50% of fully
     disclosing members charged.

  4. THE ONLY RULE THAT CATCHES THEM CATCHES EVERYBODY.
     R1 spreads the residual over every subscriber. It refuses 100%
     of splitters and charges 100% of honest single-network members,
     at 8 to 47% of their whole allowance depending on how many
     people split. That is a levy on the disclosing, and it is not
     available.

     R3's 1% wrong-charge rate is flattering and must not be quoted
     on its own: in this population the gate barely binds, so few
     honest members sit near their cap. Foundations 5.5.3 measures a
     third of people held back under the American production method.
     There, R3 fires on a third of the honest.

  5. R3 SHOWS WHY 'FLAG WHOEVER SITS AT THEIR CAP' IS NOT THE ANSWER
     EITHER. Sitting at your cap is what the gate is FOR. A rule that
     treats the cap as evidence of hiding punishes exactly the people
     using their allowance as intended.

  6. WHAT DOES SEE IT IS COVERAGE, AND ONLY IN AGGREGATE.
     Routing away lowers a network's Y and therefore its published
     Y/N -- from 74.8% down to 51.9% as splitters go from 1% to 50%
     of subscribers. The system notices. The individual is not
     caught. Coverage is a property of a network's output and was
     never a per-person instrument.

  7. THE ONE REAL BOUND IS THE SELLER, AND IT IS TEMPORARY.
     A buyer can only route to a network their seller accepts. At 5%
     of sellers on the second network the attack is worth 1.05x, not
     2.00x. That bound is not a mechanism -- it is a fact about an
     ecosystem where seller bases are unevenly split, and it fades as
     networks even out.

  ANSWER TO THE QUESTION AS ASKED. There is no split ratio at which
  the residual estimate stops closing the gap, because it never
  closes it at any ratio. The estimate is aimed at producers nobody
  measured. A splitter is measured, twice, by two books that may
  never be compared (conformance 4a).

  WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT, AND WHY IT IS NOT PROPOSED HERE. The only
  witness that separates a splitter from a frugal person is PHYSICAL
  -- what is in their home, what their meter drew. That is the
  reservoir witness of 4.4 pointed at a person, and it is exactly
  what 4.7 keeps private. Registered, not solved. It belongs with
  OP-22 (minimum audit disclosure), not with OP-24.
