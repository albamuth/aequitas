# Time as Yardstick

> Time is a convenient universal measure for summarizing material flows, but an hour is not itself value — and therefore **labor is never rate-scaled**.

## What it is

Axiom A2. Time is used because a local second is a local second everywhere, even at relativistic speed. It is the one unit needing no authority to define.

But there is **no wage rate**. No one's hour is worth 3× another's hour. Differences between workers are real, and they resolve as *material* differences:

| Difference | How it resolves |
|---|---|
| **Hard labor** | Extra caloric intake → recorded as real food-production cost |
| **Skilled labor** | **Training is credited work in its own right, discharged at the time of training.** Nothing flows downstream — a doctor's care bills the recipient for the doctor's time, clinic materials and medicines, *not* the education. See [education-as-credited-work](education-as-credited-work.md). |
| **Hazardous labor** | Health harms, once discovered, are retroactively injected as debit into the products that caused them ([retroactive-reweighting](retroactive-reweighting.md)) |

> **⚠ Amended in Foundations v0.3.** The skilled-labour row previously read *"training is a real cost that flows downstream into the debit of whoever receives the service."* That rule answered a *pricing* question and left the *incentive* question open — it made the service expensive without ever rewarding the trainee, which is exactly the failure 45 years of time-banking evidence attributes to flat-hour crediting. Front-loading dissolved OP-11 rather than solving it.

## Why it works this way

A multiplier on an hour is a **judgment**, and every judgment needs a judge. The moment you admit "a surgeon's hour is worth 8 nurse-hours," you have created an authority that sets that number — and that authority is the capture point.

Resolving skill as *material training cost* keeps the whole system on one mechanism, and it self-corrects: an expensive training pipeline makes the graduate's service genuinely dearer, which is true.

**This is the exact rock the 1827 [cost-the-limit-of-price](cost-the-limit-of-price.md) experiment broke on.** Josiah Warren could not reconcile labor-for-labor with differences in skill and unpleasantness, and finally left it to "individual judgment" — which reintroduced the judge. A2 is Aequitas's answer to a 199-year-old open problem, and it is the axiom most likely to be attacked.

## The consequence nobody noticed until OP-17

Because time is the **terminal unit**, every physical quantity in the ledger — a kilogram, a joule, a tonne of CO₂ — is already a proxy for *hours to produce* or *hours to mitigate*.

> **The universal is the denominator, not the carrier.**

This turns out to solve a problem a century of life-cycle-assessment literature could not: when one process yields several outputs, Aequitas **never has to choose between mass and energy as *the* unit of account**, because both reduce to the same thing downstream. Whichever is measurable in the case at hand is used. See [co-product-allocation](co-product-allocation.md).

**A2 was written as a defence against rate-scaling. It turned out to also be the thing that makes joint production tractable** — a stronger consequence than was recognised when the axiom was drafted.

## Open questions

- **OP-16 — the onerousness gap.** A2 resolves exertion, hazard and skill. **Tedium and indignity have no material signature**, and nothing allocates labour to jobs nobody wants. Leading candidate: hour-ceiling differentiation — pay the premium in *hours, not rate*.
- **OP-18 — labour does not allocate across co-products or across a team.** Hours-worked is a declared convention, not a measurement.
