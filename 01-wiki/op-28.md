# OP-28 — the residual denominator

> **Version:** 0.1
> **Date:** 2026-10-14

> **🟠 Open, the extent register only.** The denominator question is answered: the leftover is divided by nothing. **What is still open is that the production model which replaced the division is distributed by a declared extent, and nobody has shown how to audit it.**

## The words this page uses

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **`R = N − Y`** | The leftover: the outside total minus what subscribers recorded ([unassigned-residual](unassigned-residual.md)). |
| **`Z`** | A count of producers still unmeasured. The withdrawn denominator. |
| **Extent** | Hectares, vessel-days, throughput: a physical measure of how much of the world a producer works. |
| **Extent register** | The network's record of each unmeasured producer's extent, from which the production model distributes. |

## What was ruled

**The division was withdrawn on 2026-09-02.** §4.4 used to compute `(N − Y) ÷ Z`. The physical-trace test requires a measurement wherever the divided thing left a trace, and **land left one while a head count did not** ([physical-trace-test](physical-trace-test.md)). `06-simulation/producer-side-splitting/` put two worlds whose truths differ by **1.21×** in front of one network and handed it identical books, the same `N`, `Y`, `Z` and `R`, so no per-head rule computed from those books could describe either. The two rules that got the number right needed the other network's book, which a network does not have.

**What replaced it:** A7's per-producer estimate comes from the production model Foundations §4.1 already names, computed over the leftover and distributed by measured extent. **The rule telling a network to under-count `Z` went with the division it fed.**

## What is open

**The extent register is unaudited.** Measured on 2026-08-31:

| Perturbation | Estimate against the truth |
|---|---|
| Honest noise in the extent readings, 50% | **0.96×**, because errors cancel across many producers |
| **A producer deliberately declares half their real extent** | **1.33×**, exactly where the withdrawn rule sat |

**The counter-argument is that the survey producing `N` also catches an under-declared extent. That is an argument and not a measurement, and nobody has run it.** The survey-check model that would test it has not been built. **Until it runs, a network publishes its extent register as unaudited** (conformance row 14d).

## An example, with the numbers

**Twenty unmeasured farms, a leftover of 6,000 t, and 5,000 hectares between them, so 1.2 t/ha.** One 600-hectare farm declares 300.

| | Its estimate | What happens to the other nineteen |
|---|--:|---|
| Declared honestly | 600 × 1.2 = **720 t** | — |
| Declared at 300 ha | 300 × (6,000 ÷ 4,700) = **383 t** | The rate rises to 1.28 t/ha and **337 t lands on their neighbours** |

**In plain words: a producer halves their own estimate and pushes the difference onto the farms next door, and the arithmetic above cannot see it.** Only something that measures the land from outside could.
