# OP-10 — weighting governance

> **Version:** 0.1
> **Date:** 2026-10-14

> **🔴 Open. The top blocking problem.** Whoever sets the weighting model controls every balance in history without touching a core rule. **What survives is the original question, unchanged in size: who governs the method set.**

## The words this page uses

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Weighting model** | The numbers that turn a debit's physical quantities into hours. Each trust network runs its own. |
| **Cost constant** | One published number in that model, such as the hours to remediate a tonne of CO₂. |
| **Capture surface** | A place where controlling one thing lets a party move many figures at once. |

## What is settled

- **A side entrance is closed.** Every division of a debit happens per dimension, before collapsing ([debit-vector](debit-vector.md)). Had splits been computed on the collapsed number, the model's maintainer would have controlled every allocation in history invisibly. **This hole existed and nobody had noticed it.**
- **Cost constants have requirements.** Two unaffiliated replications before a constant re-weights history; every constant published with method, version and uncertainty interval; review triaged by magnitude times concentration of beneficiary; public membership composition; a statement of what has not been reviewed (Foundations §3.3a, conformance 16a to 16c).
- **The disparity ceiling does not depend on it.** `24 ÷ F` is counted in hours worked, so it moves with neither ρ nor the weighting model ([disparity-ceiling](disparity-ceiling.md)).
- **Exit was never the discipline; replication is.** A network's escape is non-participation, not another network, and a monopoly earned by better methods is over which method is used, never over who may propose one ([network-federation](network-federation.md)).

## What is open

**Competing open variance under A8, multiple weighting models each recomputable by anyone from the same log, is the strongest available reply. It is asserted in A8 and nowhere specified.** Three problems share this one capture surface and are worked together: a scarcity price is the dual of an optimisation, so whoever sets the objective function sets every scarcity price (OP-9); a network that can declare an activity creditable can issue credit ([evidence-rule](evidence-rule.md)); and the model itself.

**The screening question that came out of it:** *does this need an objective function?* Both allocation rules rejected for joint production, Aumann–Shapley and Kantorovich shadow prices, required one and would have re-opened OP-10 through the allocation layer. The adopted rule requires none.

## An example, with the numbers

**One network reads 1 tonne of CO₂ at 0.05 h/kg, so a stored 40 litres of petrol (92.4 kg of CO₂) weighs 4.62 hours.** Whoever can change that constant to 0.025 h/kg halves every fossil-fuel record in that network's history to 2.31 hours at once, backwards, with no rule broken. **That is the lever**, and what bounds it today is the replication requirement and the fact that a second network re-computes the same 40 litres through its own constant ([evidence-rule](evidence-rule.md)).
