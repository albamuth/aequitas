# No Externalities

> Every consequence of an activity is priced into it, including consequences discovered decades later. There is no "outside" of the accounting.

## What it is

Axiom A4. In conventional economics an externality is a cost that falls on someone who isn't party to the transaction — pollution, occupational disease, road wear, resource depletion. Economists treat these as *market failures* to be corrected by an outside authority (a tax, a cap, a regulator).

In Aequitas there is no outside authority because there is no outside. If an activity moved matter or energy, it is in the log ([[material-flow-value]]). Whether we have *measured* it yet is a separate question, answered by [[retroactive-reweighting]].

## Why it works this way

Externalities exist only because conventional accounting has a boundary — the firm, the transaction, the fiscal year. Aequitas's boundary is the physical world, which has no outside.

This inverts the incentive gradient without any regulation:
- Exploitative labor and pollution make a product **dearer**, not cheaper.
- Cleaning up is the cheapest way to lower your product's debit-cost.
- The regulator stops being an adversary ([[regulator-inversion]]).

Note the honest limitation: **A4 is a claim about the model, not about measurement.** Unmeasured harm is still unpriced harm until someone measures it. What A4 guarantees is that when it *is* measured, the correction propagates backwards through all history. That is a weaker but far more defensible claim, and it should be stated that way in public writing.
