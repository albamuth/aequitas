# No Taxation

> **Version:** 0.1
> **Date:** 2026-09-16

> Taxation is unnecessary — not abolished, but left with nothing to collect and nothing to fund.

## What it is

Taxation exists to do three jobs. Aequitas removes all three:

| Job of taxation | Why it's unnecessary |
|---|---|
| **Fund public servants** | A civil servant's work is a [service](service-credit.md) to the people who consented to it. The worker is credited, and those people carry the hours in equal parts. There is no payroll to finance. |
| **Fund infrastructure** | The work of keeping a road up is a service of the same kind, shared the same way. The road's creation cost stays on its holders, who are backed by the people who pledged for it ([front-loading-rule](front-loading-rule.md)). |
| **Correct externalities** (Pigouvian) | [no-externalities](no-externalities.md) — harm is already priced in. Nothing left to correct. |
| **Redistribute** | [debit-tolerance](debit-tolerance.md) provides the floor structurally, not by transfer. |

There is nothing to collect because there is no fungible thing that could be collected ([non-fungibility](non-fungibility.md)).

## An example, with the numbers

A city worker mows verges for **1,000 hours** in a year. **10,000 residents** consented to the city's services. *(Illustrative digits, from Foundations §5.4.)*

| | |
|---|--:|
| The worker is credited | **1,000 h** |
| Each consenting resident carries | 1,000 ÷ 10,000 = **0.1 h** |
| A visitor who walks on the mown verge carries | **0 h** |

**No credit moves from a resident to the city.** The resident's part is a record of debit, like any other. A person who benefits without consenting carries nothing, because a debit lands on a person only by their own act or their acceptance (Foundations §2.4a, the consent rule).

**Three things are left to the network and the municipality.**

| | Whose decision |
|---|---|
| What counts as consent to a city's bundle of services | The published rule of the network and the municipality |
| Which services are essential, and so add no debit to anybody | The network's published list. A fire service is a likely entry |
| How many services a city runs, and how much of each | Government and local law |

**The same work with no consenting receivers is not credited.** A mower whom residents only pledged for, and whom nobody asked to receive, earns the pledged hours as backing ([pledge-and-signal](pledge-and-signal.md)).

## Why it works this way

Every one of taxation's functions is a workaround for accounting that doesn't close. Fix the accounting and the workarounds have no remaining job.

Note this is *not* a libertarian claim. Aequitas keeps municipal government, planning bodies, and civil service fully intact — see the positioning: **change the economic nature of institutions, not the institutions.** Public services don't shrink; they stop being funded by extraction and start being credited directly.

## Who games this

The obvious question a skeptic asks: **who decides how many civil servants to credit?** Foundations §5.4 leaves the number of services to government and local law, and puts the hours on the residents who consented. That says who carries the hours. It does not say how much service is authorized. Without an allocation mechanism this becomes either unlimited (inflationary in real terms) or centrally decided (an authority, contra A8).

This is [service-credit](service-credit.md) / OP-1 again, and it means **§5.4's claim is currently downstream of the project's largest unsolved problem.** Don't present it as settled in public writing until OP-1 resolves.
