# Markets vs Antimarkets

> Manuel DeLanda's argument, drawn from Fernand Braudel: **capitalism and the market are different entities**, and capitalism has always been the *anti*-market.

## The claim

Braudel found two distinct layers in economic history, not one:

| Layer | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Market** | Many small producers. Price emerges from below through real competition. Participants are **price-takers**. Market towns are the type case. |
| **Antimarket** | Large firms, monopoly of long-distance trade, financial speculation. Price is **imposed from above**. Internally coordinated by managerial command, not price signal. |

DeLanda's summary: capitalism "has always engaged in anti-competitive practices, manipulating demand and supply in a variety of ways," and inside large hierarchies "prices are increasingly replaced by commands as coordinating mechanisms."

He also notes the direction of innovation runs the other way from the folk story: *"It was not the capitalists who brought about the first cotton revolution; all the new ideas came from enterprising small businesses."*

## Why this matters to Aequitas

**It names the actual target.** Aequitas is positioned against oligarchic capture, not against exchange, enterprise, or institutions that work. DeLanda supplies the historical argument that these were never the same thing.

Concretely:

1. **It rescues the framing.** [[cost-not-price]] does not abolish the market — it abolishes the price-*setter*. Small-producer markets under real competition already tend toward selling at cost; Aequitas makes that structural rather than contingent.
2. **It supplies the rhetorical position.** Aequitas can credibly claim to be *pro-market and anti-capitalist*, with historical evidence rather than assertion. See [[capitalism-cannot-function]].
3. **It matches the meshwork/hierarchy distinction** to Aequitas's own decentralization criterion — DeLanda argues synergistic properties in markets, ecosystems, and decentralized networks "emerge spontaneously," which is precisely the fecundity claim.

## Where it doesn't help

DeLanda diagnoses; he prescribes nothing. There is no accounting mechanism here, no answer to how prices form absent both market and command. Aequitas supplies that. Treat this as **framing ammunition, not theory.**

Also: the market/antimarket line is contested among economic historians and is a *tendency* claim, not a clean taxonomy. Don't over-claim it.
