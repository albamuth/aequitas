# OP-22 — minimum audit disclosure

> **Version:** 0.1
> **Date:** 2026-10-14

> **🟠 Open, and load-bearing.** The question is not *surveillance or privacy*. It is **what is the least an auditor must see to check a claim without seeing a history?** Proofs that reveal nothing but the answer are the right shape. **The precise set of what must be disclosed does not exist yet**, and the guard against a lax network exporting inflated credit is only as real as that set.

## The words this page uses

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Disclosure set** | The minimum an auditor must be shown to verify a claim such as *"this pledge is drawn from X hours recorded under weighting model M."* |
| **Comparison, never conversion** | A second network re-computes a claim from the shared physical record through its own model ([evidence-rule](evidence-rule.md)). Re-computing what a claim is drawn from means seeing it. |
| **Lifetime reconstruction** | A joining person's position rebuilt back to birth ([lifetime-reconstruction](lifetime-reconstruction.md)). |
| **C6** | The identity requirement: a network can tell one verified human from another. **Not part of OP-22**, by ruling. |

## What is settled

- **Who decides is settled: the trust network.** It does the tallying, so it holds what is private and sets the practice, from a card-scheme-style intermediary to complete transparency ([privacy-is-a-network-choice](privacy-is-a-network-choice.md)). A global privacy constant would be the central authority A8 forbids.
- **The split is refused** (author ruling 2026-08-25). A critic asked for OP-22a and OP-22b, with a *non-reuse witness* that one account is not two. That witness is C6, identity, and a precondition of merging two networks. It is not a disclosure primitive ([one-account-per-person](one-account-per-person.md)).
- **The cross-network ceiling clause was struck**, not narrowed: the disparity ceiling is one network's books, and two networks' figures are never added.
- **Opacity is discounted rather than forbidden.** A counterparty discounts what it cannot check, so a heavily opaque network finds its members' claims trade at a discount.
- **Privacy's coverage cost is measured and not close.** The dark pool still unravels to 0.1% at a disclosure cost of 0.40 against a median unit near 1.0; realistic costs sit near 0.002. **The failure point is about 200 times higher than anything a working network would report.**

## What is open

1. **The disclosure set itself.** No mechanism exists. Public pseudonymous events can be analysed to identify a person, which is the classic ledger-privacy problem.
2. **The dossier.** A whole-life record is birthplace, every residence, employment, commuting distance, vehicles owned. Disclosure is voluntary, but the incentive runs toward disclosing, so the arrangement puts steady pressure on people to assemble exactly the record a surveillance state would want. §4.7's split of public market from private person now has to hold across a lifetime.
3. **Publishing more to earn trust publishes more to identify people by.** Anonymised participant data becomes more re-identifiable the more of it there is.
4. **The network becomes the most information-rich actor in the system**, holding a concentration of information comparable to the concentration of wealth this project removes. Scoped out with reasons in `C2_information_capture.md`; the watch item is that a merit monopoly can stop being meritorious.

## An example, with the numbers

**A pledge of 2,000 hours is offered across a model boundary.** The receiving network must check that the pledger earned at least 2,000 hours.

| What the auditor could be shown | What it reveals | Does it exist? |
|---|---|---|
| The pledger's whole log | 40 years of residences, jobs and purchases | Yes, and it is the dossier |
| A proof that *C ≥ 2,000 h under model M*, and nothing else | Only the answer | **This is OP-22. Not yet** |

**In plain words: the two things leaning on this, the market check on a lax network and the lifetime reconstruction, are only as safe as a mechanism nobody has built.**
