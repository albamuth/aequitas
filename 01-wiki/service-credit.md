# Service Credit

> Credit for labor that produces no object — care, teaching, medicine, civil service. It converts not to holdings but to **responsibility and influence**.

## What it is

The second credit type. A nurse, a teacher, a bus driver, and a city planner all move matter and energy and all leave no artifact to hold. Production credit's "raise your ceiling" answer doesn't fit; the person's output went into other people.

**This is a genuine gap requiring design work — OP-1, the most important unsolved piece in Aequitas.**

## Three candidate mechanisms

| Candidate | How it works | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain-scoped voting weight** | Service credit in transit weighs on transit decisions only. Expertise-weighted democracy, non-transferable across domains. | Domain boundaries become the capture point. Who defines "transit"? |
| **Stewardship eligibility** | Accumulated service unlocks the *right to hold* coordinating and planning roles. Influence via office. | Reinvents a credentialed civil-service class; incumbents define the ladder. |
| **Proposal power with universal suffrage** | High service credit grants the right to *propose*; everyone affected votes equally. Separates agenda-setting from deciding. | Agenda control is real power. Also the most defensible of the three. |

These must be developed and reconciled, not chosen by taste.

## Why it is hard

Every candidate converts material accounting into **political power**, and that conversion is the exact place an oligarchy could regrow — this time credentialed rather than moneyed. Aequitas exists to remove oligarchic capture; a mechanism that installs a new one fails on its own terms.

Test any proposal against: *can a group with high service credit in one domain entrench itself?* If yes, it's wrong.
