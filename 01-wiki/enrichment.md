# Enrichment

> **Version:** 0.6
> **Date:** 2026-11-19

> **Enrichment is knowledge, skill or culture that a person gains or passes on. It benefits society as a whole, also where it seems to benefit one person only** (Foundations A1). An apprentice who trains as a plumber gains a trade, and society gains a plumber. A child in the fifth year of school is becoming a person who can contribute to society. **What a person does only for their own rest or pleasure is not work.** Teaching, learning, research, writing, art, childcare. It is one of three names a trust network uses to decide whether an hour counts as work. It is not a kind of credit and it is not a social-value signal. **There is one credit, time spent, and an enrichment hour credits exactly as any other hour does.**

**A network ratifies the kinds of enrichment that it credits.** To ratify a kind of work is to publish an evidence rule for it. Much enrichment has no receiver who confirms the work, so the network's rule is what decides. **Aequitas does not say which kinds a network ratifies, and it does not say how a network decides** (Foundations §2.3). Three examples follow, and none is a rule of Aequitas.

- **Study.** A network can write its rule for study on accreditation, which is a system that checks schools and recognises the ones whose teaching and degrees are sound. Under that rule, a student at a recognised school who studies for 1,000 hours is credited 1,000 hours. A student at a school that no accreditor on the network's list recognises is credited 0 hours. **Accreditation can be captured:** the accreditor [ACICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accrediting_Council_for_Independent_Colleges_and_Schools) recognised colleges that collapsed in 2015 and 2016, and lost its federal recognition in 2022.
- **Schooling on the essential list.** A network decides what work is essential, and it can count a kind of enrichment. Schooling for young children is the likely case. A teacher teaches 25 children for 5 hours. Each child is credited 5 hours of study, the teacher is credited 5 hours, and nobody is debited: 130 hours of credit and 0 of debit. **This is the essential list used as a lever.** Two adults who each claim 7 hours of teaching the other and 7 of study gain 0.330 × 14 × 365 = 1,686 hours a year each at ρ = 0.330.
- **Research.** It works by pledges. A network holds a research fund as a bank holds the money for a prize or a grant, and it decides when the pledged hours are released to a project. 200 people who pledge 5 hours each to the fund give a researcher 1,000 hours of backing and no credit. Research that only a special interest wants is done under that interest's pledges. A network can also define a kind of research as essential. It then credits the work in that field, with no pledge fund and no debit beside the credit.

**A special interest can control what a network does.** Its fund can release pledged hours to research that many pledgers do not support, and its essential list can hold training that serves it. What limits this is that joining is voluntary, networks are many, and the rules are published. Those limits work only as far as a subscriber can leave (Foundations §2.3).

## The words this page uses

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Credit** | A record that a person spent an hour of their life on work. It belongs to one person and never moves ([non-fungibility](non-fungibility.md)). |
| **The three names** | [Production](production-credit.md), [service](service-credit.md) and enrichment. An activity is creditable if it is at least one of the three (Foundations §2.3). |
| **Feedback** | How a society signals what it wants more of: a stock-out, a referral, applause, a like. **It is not credit and never converts into credit** (Foundations §4.6). |
| **Pledge** | Permanent backing of a named person, drawn one hour for one hour from credit the pledger already earned ([pledge-and-signal](pledge-and-signal.md)). |

## What it is

**Enrichment is named separately for one reason: to give grounds for crediting work no economy has ever credited.** Going to school is work, and today the student or their parents pay for it. Teaching your own child is work. Caring for a relative is work. None of it is paid today, and all of it passes the threshold (Foundations §2.3). **Childcare alone brings the largest uncounted labour pool in history onto the books** (Foundations §4.5).

**The name decides whether an hour counts. It never decides how much.** An hour of teaching and an hour of bricklaying both credit one hour (A2). An apprentice plumber's hour is production, a service and enrichment at once, and it credits as **1 hour**, not 3.

**How it is checked.** A network publishes what evidence each kind of work needs (Foundations §4.2).

| The enrichment | The evidence |
|---|---|
| **Given to other people**: a lesson, a lecture, a concert | **A receiver who asked for it confirms it**, as for a [service](service-credit.md). One confirming receiver is enough |
| **Other enrichment**: a paper, private study | Evidence that the work happened, under the network's published rule |
| **Essential care**: work that continues another person's life, such as feeding and washing an infant | The network's published rule for that kind of care. The receiver does not have to confirm it. Where the receiver cannot confirm, a second witness does |

**Worked.** A 1-hour lecture to 500 people who asked for it credits the lecturer **1 hour**. Each of the 500 carries 1 ÷ 500 = **0.002 hours**. A violinist who plays alone has given no concert. A band that plays in a park, with no gate and no tickets, has no receiver either. Those hours are recorded, and they do not count as credit (Foundations §4.5, §4.6).

> **Verification asks whether the work happened, and whether a receiver accepted it. It never asks how much anyone valued it.** Letting credit depend on likes, citations or applause is forbidden, because it would make feedback a currency by the back door (Foundations §4.2, §4.6).

## Feedback is not credit, and there is nothing to firewall

**Feedback was never credit, so it cannot convert into credit.** A like, a review, a referral or a stock-out tells a society what it wants more of. The thing with real hours behind it is a pledge, and a pledge is spent from a finite lifetime budget equal to the pledger's own earned hours.

### An example, with the numbers

**An artist spends 40 hours painting a street mural and posts a photograph. 5,000 people like it.**

| | Hours |
|---|--:|
| Credit for the 40 hours of painting, once the network's evidence rule is met | **40 h**, and no more however many likes arrive |
| Credit from the 5,000 likes | **0 h.** A like is feedback |
| Backing raised, if each liker's app pledges 0.1 h from their own budget | 5,000 × 0.1 = **500 h** of backing for the artist |
| What the next mural needs in paint and travel | **300 h** |
| Left over | **200 h** of backing the artist can use |

**In plain words: the likes turned into backing only because 5,000 people each spent a tenth of an hour of their own finite pledge budget.** Nobody's credit moved, and the artist's 40 hours did not grow. That is the universal rule for how popularity reaches a creator, and it is the same whether the work is a mural, a film, or a theorem.

**The same rule pays for creative work up front.** A film's crew are credited during production, and the people who wanted the film pledge for it then. The audience pays for delivery only ([education-as-credited-work](education-as-credited-work.md) states the front-loading rule). **A popular film cannot gouge its audience, because there is no mechanism by which it could** (Overview §5).

## Who games this

- **Stretching the word.** A network cannot declare loyalty to itself enrichment, because loyalty passes none of the three names. **It can still stretch enrichment a long way**, and nothing here stops it. Three structural defences carry the rest: competing networks and ratio-based evaluation, a public membership list, and the published evidence rule. This is **OP-10 (weighting governance)** wearing different clothes (Foundations §2.3).
- **Claiming thinking hours nobody can see.** Recorded, as A7 requires, but weighed near zero until something corroborates it, and capped by IC-7 at 24 hours a day (Foundations §4.5).
- **Buying feedback.** If a signal could be bought it would be a currency by the back door. Whether feedback can be bought is **OP-6 (feedback mechanics)** and is open. What bounds it is that a pledge costs a real person a real hour from a budget that cannot be manufactured.
- **A popularity contest.** Whoever is already liked attracts the most pledges. That is OP-6 too, registered and not solved. A first-time creator attracts no pledges, and the documents say so rather than assuming it away (Foundations §4.5).
