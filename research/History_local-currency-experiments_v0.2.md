## Why this matters to Aequitas

Two other notes ([Warren: cost the limit of price](../docs/GLOSSARY.md#src-warren-cost-the-limit-of-price) and [Proudhon: mutualism](../docs/GLOSSARY.md#src-proudhon-mutualism)) cover the *thinkers* who came before Aequitas. This note covers the *field record* — roughly a century of people actually building alternative-money systems and finding out what breaks them. It's the closest thing the project has to an experimental literature.

The hunch going in was that Ithaca and Burlington failed for the same reason Warren did. **That's half right, and the other half is more useful.** Warren and Proudhon died of a *pricing* problem (how to value an hour of skilled versus unskilled work). Ithaca and Burlington died of a *circulation* problem — the money physically piling up somewhere and stopping — which is a completely different failure, and one Aequitas is structurally immune to. Meanwhile the time-banking studies give **hard, recent, real-world confirmation of the pricing problem** at a scale Warren never could.

## Three distinct ways these things die

The record doesn't show one failure on repeat. It shows three.

### Death #1 — Pricing: a flat hour can't recruit skilled people

- **Warren (1827–30):** couldn't reconcile "an hour for an hour" with differences in skill and unpleasantness; gave up and called it "a matter of individual judgment."
- **Time banking (1980– ):** these systems value **every hour identically, no matter who works it.** The [2025 PLOS One review](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0322760) finds the predictable result — a **skills shortage**: particular skills stay scarce, there's no guarantee the bank can actually meet anyone's needs, and it also documents people *hoarding* their earned hours rather than spending them.

**This is the most forceful finding in the whole note.** Warren's failure can be dismissed as one man in one shop in 1830. Time banking is *forty-five years, dozens of countries, and a peer-reviewed literature* — and flat-hour crediting *still* fails to recruit skilled labour. The worry about crediting every hour equally is no longer theoretical.

Aequitas's answer is genuinely different and worth stating precisely: it does **not** pay skilled work at a higher rate. Instead, **the cost of training is paid up front** — by the people who wanted those skilled workers to exist — and never charged to whoever later uses the skill. A doctor and a gardener are both credited one hour per hour worked; the doctor's *training* was simply carried at the time, by its sponsors, rather than billed to patients later. See [Foundations §6.2 (training, front-loaded)](../docs/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.16.md#62-training-front-loaded).

**That fixes the pricing side but leaves a motivation question open:** why spend ten years training if your hour credits the same afterward? Aequitas's honest answer is that this is bound up with its unsolved "unpleasant/undesirable work" problem — see [Foundations §10](../docs/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.16.md#10-open-problems). Time banking is the field evidence that leaving it untreated is fatal.

### Death #2 — Circulation: the money pools at a dead end and stops

This is what actually killed both of the systems people usually name, by the same mechanism each time.

- **Ithaca HOURS.** Founded November 1991 by Paul Glover, pegged at **1 HOUR = US$10** (the county's average hourly wage). At its peak: **1,000+ people and 500 businesses**, including a hospital and a credit union. As the network shrank, the remaining businesses **"were drowning in Hours"** — taking in far more than they could spend.
- **Burlington Bread.** Ran 1997–2007, up to **20,000 "slices"** in circulation. Bread **piled up at a few cafés** that had no way to spend it back out.

The mechanism is about *where the money can flow*, not about how it's valued. Local scrip drifts toward businesses whose own supplies come from *outside* the network — a café buys its coffee from Colombia in dollars, so dollars-worth of scrip flows in and none flows out. Those businesses are one-way drains. Circulation stops, and it stops fastest at exactly the eager early adopters.

**Contrast the two systems that *didn't* die:**

| | Ithaca / Burlington | WIR / Sardex |
|---|---|---|
| Who trades | shops selling to consumers | **businesses selling to each other, in tight loops** |
| Form of money | printed paper scrip | **mutual credit — everyone starts at zero, "money" is created in the act of a trade and cancelled as it's repaid** |
| Risk of pooling | high — shops buy supplies outside | low — businesses buy from one another |
| Result | dead in ~15 and ~10 years | **WIR: 1934–present, ~60,000 businesses; Sardex: 4,000+ businesses, ~€50m/yr** |

WIR also grows precisely when regular money is scarce (it's *countercyclical*), which is why it has lasted ninety years. Sardex was deliberately modelled on early WIR.

### Death #3 — Institutional: burnout, the state, and obsolescence

- **Dependence on one enthusiast.** Ithaca's decline is blamed first on **Glover moving away.** He himself said every local currency needs at least one full-time person to "promote, facilitate and troubleshoot."
- **Being shut down by the state.** Wörgl's scrip (which lost 1% of its value per month if not spent, to keep it moving) cut local unemployment **16% while Austria's rose 19%**, and funded a reservoir, houses, a bridge, and a ski jump — then was **shut down by Austria's central bank in September 1933** to protect the national-currency monopoly. **It was killed for working.**
- **Going obsolete.** HOURS stayed on paper and never made it to electronic payment; a 2019 digital revival failed.

## What we can use

**1. Aequitas is structurally immune to Death #2 — and hasn't claimed the credit.**

There's no "money pools at a dead end" problem in a system with **no money to pool.** Credit records who did the work and never moves; only debit moves, and only stuck to the object it belongs to (see [Foundations §A3 (non-fungibility)](../docs/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.16.md#a3-non-fungibility)). Nobody can drown in credit they can't spend, because nobody ever *receives* credit from anyone. The exact failure that ended Ithaca and Burlington **cannot happen in Aequitas** — and it falls out of an existing rule, not a new mechanism. Say so plainly. See [Foundations §7.6](../docs/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.16.md#76-why-the-alternative-economy-graveyard-does-not-apply).

**2. Wörgl proves why the "don't call it a currency" rule matters.**

Wörgl was shut down because it was a **currency** competing with legal tender. Aequitas is an accounting system with no issuer, no notes, and nothing to counterfeit — so the legal weapon that killed Wörgl doesn't fit it, and there's no office to raid. The project's rule against ever calling Aequitas a "currency" isn't branding fussiness — *this is the strategic reason.*

**3. WIR and Sardex tell you what a first deployment should look like.**

Both survived by starting **business-to-business, in dense supply loops** — not with retail shoppers — and both grow when regular money dries up. If Aequitas ever needs a first *real* pilot, the field record points to a tight cluster of businesses that supply each other, launched during a downturn. See [Foundations §11 (the MVP)](../docs/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.16.md#11-first-foothold--the-mvp).

**4. "Does this need a Paul Glover?" — a permanent screening question.**

Aequitas's requirement that a system *sustain itself* is exactly the test Ithaca failed: it needed a full-time evangelist and died when he left. Every proposed mechanism should be asked whether it secretly depends on one dedicated enthusiast. Aequitas's answer — **auditing and coordination are themselves credited work** — is the right *shape*, because it pays the maintainer from inside the system rather than relying on a volunteer.

## Where Aequitas is exposed

1. **All of these were pegged to, and dependent on, national money.** One HOUR was *defined* as $10; Bread mirrored dollars. They were never really independent — they were national currency with a local-loyalty sticker, which is why they added little and faded. Aequitas's first-deployment idea (a parallel overlay on existing commerce) is a hair away from the same trap. The saving difference is that Aequitas's overlay computes a number money *can't* produce — a thing's true physical cost — and any pilot should say so explicitly, to distinguish itself from this entire graveyard rather than resemble it.
2. **Aequitas has a cousin of the pooling problem, one level up.** Being immune to money *pooling* isn't the same as being immune to *saturation*. A very productive person eventually piles up credit that lifts a ceiling they're nowhere near — extra credit that does nothing for them. Ithaca's shops drowned in HOURS they couldn't spend; Aequitas's most productive people could drown in headroom they don't want. Capitalism's answer is accumulation, which Aequitas forbids by design. The intended answer is that surplus producers get to *direct what the world works on next* (via pledges and recognition) — which makes that recognition channel load-bearing, not decorative. See [Foundations §6.3](../docs/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.16.md#63-feedback-what-each-channel-looks-like).
3. **Matching failure in small groups.** Time banks and LETS both suffer the "nobody nearby offers what you need" problem below a certain size. Aequitas is less exposed — it *prices* rather than *matches* — but the rule letting a low-tech community trade with a high-tech one, and cross-community trade generally, inherits the issue, because a small community is exactly a small network. See [Foundations §4 (verification ladder)](../docs/Aequitas_Foundations_v0.16.md#4-verification--the-four-level-ladder).
4. **Hoarding shows up even without interest.** Time-bankers bank hours against future need despite there being no interest and nothing to gain by waiting. Worth noting against any assumption that non-transferability removes all hoarding urges. Bears on the design of the personal debit allowance.
5. **Scale is unproven everywhere.** The biggest success here is WIR at ~60,000 businesses *inside* a working franc economy. Nothing in the record tests a *whole* economy, so cite these as evidence about *failure modes* — never as proof Aequitas will scale.
