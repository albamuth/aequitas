## 3. How to finish the three `[WIP]` sections

### §03 — *Solution: Credit*
Three moves, no jargon:
1. **A credit is a record of an hour someone actually worked** — not a claim on anyone, not a token.
2. **Credit can't move.** It isn't yours to give, because it's a fact about what *you* did. So nobody is ever owed, and no debt spiral can start — the thing that makes a modern debt crisis a crisis is a creditor who must be made whole, and there is none.
3. **The books never balance, and shouldn't.** Every real process wastes something; total debit always exceeds total credit. That's not a hole in the accounting, it's thermodynamics being honest. Two numbers matter: **how much you contributed**, and **how much you consumed per unit contributed**.

*This section is where the v0.1 "Problem: Debt" quotation finally pays off — it was left dangling.*

### §04 — *Problem: Gambling → Solution: Infungibility*
v0.1 contained only filler here. Proposed content:
- **The problem is broader than casinos.** Anything fungible can be bet, hoarded, cornered, lent at interest, or rented out. Money's interchangeability is exactly the feature that lets it be gambled — and lets a landlord earn without working.
- **Solution:** every credit is a record of a specific event by a specific person. **There is no chip to push across the table.** Not banned — *unavailable*.
- Consequences worth naming for a lay reader: **no theft of credit, no rent, no interest, no speculation, no inflation.** All one property, not five rules.
- **What survives:** genuine risk. Building something nobody wants still wastes real effort. Enterprise stays risky; only the *extraction* is gone.

### §05 — *Problem: Intellectual Property → Solution: Meme Tracing*
- **The problem:** patents and copyright exist to let a holder charge for copies. They convert an idea — the one thing in the universe that costs nothing to duplicate — into artificial scarcity.
- **Why it evaporates here:** with no profit in exchange (A5) there is no revenue stream for exclusion to protect. **The machinery has nothing left to do.**
- **Who pays for creation, then?** Pledges, up front, during the making. A film's viewers pay only for **delivery** — the projector, the power, the bandwidth. A blockbuster cannot gouge its audience because there is no mechanism to.
- **Meme tracing:** ideas are traced as they spread, so originators get *recognition* — which converts into pledges for their next work, and **never into credit**.
- **State the honest standard:** attribution is held to *no worse than today*, not *perfect*. Buy a painting today and there's no video of its making either.
- **State the honest limit:** the "recording advertises the live show" argument works for music and poorly for writing, software, and research. Say so.
